## 2. #1477, slice 4a: the `mail` queue, the rate limiter, the second worker, the budget alert

### 2.1 What the provider says (Resend documentation, read on 2026-11-16)

1. The API limit is 10 requests per second per team, shared by all API keys of the team. A request over the limit gets 429. The response headers are `ratelimit-limit`, `ratelimit-remaining`, `ratelimit-reset` and `retry-after`. Source: resend.com/docs/api-reference/rate-limit.
2. The monthly quota applies to all plans and counts sent and received mail. The daily quota applies only to the Free plan and resets at midnight UTC. Over a quota the API answers 429 with `daily_quota_exceeded` or `monthly_quota_exceeded`. Same source.
3. Plans (resend.com/pricing, read today, may change): Free 3 000 mails a month and 100 a day; Pro 50 000 a month at 20 USD; Pro 100 000 at 35 USD; Scale from 100 000. Pro and Scale bill extra mail by blocks of 1 000 only when "Transactional Overages" is on in the team settings; otherwise sending is refused over the quota.
4. The usage endpoint gives `emails.monthly` and `emails.daily` with `used`, `limit`, `sent`, `received` and `resets_at`. The month is a calendar month, and the sample shows `resets_at` on the first of the next month at 00:00 UTC. Source: resend.com/docs/api-reference/usage/retrieve-usage. The SDK has the same fields in `vendor/resend/resend-php/src/Usage.php`.
5. Not confirmed: whether the usage endpoint exists on every plan; whether the quota of the team is shared with other products that send with the same team (expected: yes, "per team"); the exact text of the error message that the transport gives on a 429 (the transport copies the message of Resend, but no real message was seen).
6. The estimate of design 6.14 point 12 (3 500 mails a month at expected use, 16 000 in the worst case) is above the Free plan (3 000 a month). The operator of an instance must know its plan (see decision 1).

### 2.2 Which mail goes to the queue `mail`

1. Proposal: only notification mail goes to `mail`. That is every mail that slice 4c and 5 add (level 1 and level 2 mail, the weekly summary, the care mail). The three existing mails stay on `default` and are not changed.
2. Reason 1: design 6.13 point 6 says the existing mails are level 0 and are "never held". A burst of 400 notification mails at 08:00 (see 6.2 of the design) must not make a sign-in link wait behind a rate limiter.
3. Reason 2: the limiter is for volume. The existing mails are a handful a day.
4. Reason 3: a change to the three existing mails would touch three actions and their tests for no gain in slice 4a.
5. The cost: the limiter does not see the existing mails, so the provider limit is shared by two lanes. The default limit is set to half of the provider limit (see 2.3) to leave room. The budget counter (2.5) counts every mail, whatever the lane, because it listens to the send event and not to the queue.
6. Risk to know: `default` is the last queue of the first worker, so a long backlog on `notifications` already delays a sign-in link today. This is not new and slice 4a does not fix it. A later option is to send the sign-in link on its own queue. Put it in the list of open points (section 6).
7. Alternative: send all mail through `mail`. It is one rule, easy to explain. Not recommended, because of reasons 1 and 2.
8. The name of the queue is a constant `Constants::QUEUE_MAIL = 'mail'`, and a value `MAIL_QUEUE` (default `mail`) in `config/mail.php` lets an operator who does not want a second process write `default`. The mailables read the config, not the constant (see 2.3). This is the way for an instance that cannot run two workers. It is documented in `SELF_HOSTING.md` as a downgrade: the limiter then runs on the first worker.

### 2.3 The rate limiter

1. Where the limit comes from: a configuration value, because it depends on the provider and on the plan. `config/mail.php` gets a key `rate_limit_per_second` read from `MAIL_RATE_LIMIT_PER_SECOND`. Default 5, which is half of the 10 requests a second of Resend. The value 0 means no limit (for `log`, for an own SMTP server).
2. The name of the limiter is `mail`. It is registered in `AppServiceProvider` with `RateLimiter::for('mail', ...)` and returns `Limit::perSecond($n)->by('mail')` or `Limit::none()` when the value is 0. `Limit::perSecond` exists in the vendored Laravel (`Cache/RateLimiting/Limit.php` line 63). It has one global key, so two mail workers would share the same count (it runs on the cache, which is the database; the count is approximate under concurrency, which is fine for a limit set at half of the real one).
3. The middleware is `Illuminate\Queue\Middleware\RateLimited('mail')`. The vendored version (`Queue/Middleware/RateLimited.php`) releases the job back to the queue for `availableIn + 3` seconds when the limit is reached. A released job is not failed and the worker does not sleep.
4. The base class: one abstract `App\Mail\NotificationMail extends Mailable` (name to settle with `documentation/conventions/naming.md`) that sets `onQueue(config('mail.queue'))`, returns the middleware from `middleware()` (`Mailable::newQueuedJob` reads it: `vendor/laravel/framework/src/Illuminate/Mail/Mailable.php` line 299), and fixes the retry rules (next point). Every notification mailable of 4c extends it.
5. What a job does when the limit is reached: it is released. The danger is the tries counter. A released job counts one attempt, so with `--tries=3` (production) three releases would fail a job that never failed. The fix, from the vendored `Queue/Worker.php` line 790: when a job has `retryUntil`, the tries limit is ignored. So the base class sets `retryUntil()` (proposal: 2 hours after queueing for a notification mail; a mail that is 2 hours late is no longer the mail of design 6.2) and `maxExceptions = 3` (only real exceptions count) and a `backoff()` of 60, 300 and 1 800 seconds. `SendQueuedMailable` reads all of these from the mailable (`SendQueuedMailable.php` lines 96 to 144). To check: a test that releases a job five times and still sends it once.
6. The state is read again when the job runs (design 6.2 point 5, 6.14 point 8). With a limiter this matters more: a job can wait. The check is in `handle()` after the middleware, so a released job has read nothing yet.
7. How a retry stays harmless: two cases.
   1. Release by the limiter: the middleware runs before `handle()`, so nothing was claimed or sent. A release is harmless by construction.
   2. A real failure or a crash after the provider accepted the mail: the job could send twice. The delivery log of design 6.14 point 8 handles it: a unique index on `(notification_id, channel)`. Proposal: the job inserts its row with `insertOrIgnore` and the status `sending` before it sends. If the insert adds no row, a row exists: status `sent` or `sending` means "stop" (at most once). When the transport throws, the job sets the row to `failed` so that the next try may claim it again. A crash between the send and the update leaves `sending`, and the mail is counted as sent: one possible lost mail, never a double. The notification stays in the app and goes to "About you" (design 6.2 point 7). This favours "no double" over "no loss" and that is right for notification mail. Alternative: the Resend idempotency key header. To check: whether Resend supports it and for how long. It is not needed with the at-most-once rule.
8. The delivery log table does not exist. It is needed by 4b (see 3.5), not by 4a alone. Put its migration in the first PR of 4b (section 5) and let slice 4c use it. Slice 4a needs no row: it has no notification mail yet (see 5.1 for the test).
9. A refusal by the provider because of a quota (429 with `daily_quota_exceeded` or `monthly_quota_exceeded`): the job must not retry in a loop. Proposal: `NotificationMail::failed()` and a small catch in the base class detect the two codes in the message (to check the exact text), set a "hold" in the cache until the quota resets, and end the job without error. A hold is described in 2.5.

### 2.4 The second worker

1. The exact supervisor program for `docker/8.5/supervisord.conf`, next to `queue_worker`:

```ini
[program:queue_worker_mail]
# The queue "mail" has its own worker (design 6.14, ADR 0042): a slow or blocked send never holds up the live updates
command=php /var/www/html/artisan queue:work --queue=mail
autostart=true
autorestart=true
# Keep retrying while the database or Redis restarts (the default gives up after 3 quick failures)
startretries=100
user=%(ENV_SUPERVISOR_PHP_USER)s
stdout_logfile=/dev/stdout
stdout_logfile_maxbytes=0
stderr_logfile=/dev/stderr
stderr_logfile_maxbytes=0
```

2. The first program is unchanged: `--queue=broadcasts,notifications,default`. It must not read `mail`. That keeps the order `broadcasts,notifications,default` that ADR 0042 point 26 fixed, and it keeps a slow send away from the live updates. The second worker reads `mail` only.
3. Why a second worker and not one more name in the first list: `smtp` in `config/mail.php` has `timeout => null`, so a send to a slow SMTP server can block a worker for a long time. With one worker, that would stop `broadcasts`. This is the whole reason for the design, so it stays. (A released job does not block a worker, so the limiter alone is not a reason.)
4. The other places that carry the worker command, which the issue does not list: `compose.production.yaml` (add a service `queue-mail` with the same image and `command: ['php', 'artisan', 'queue:work', '--queue=mail', '--sleep=3']`; to check: the health check `knowii-healthcheck-process queue:work` finds a process named `queue:work`, so it may report healthy while the mail worker is dead; the check may need the queue name), `CONTRIBUTING.md` line 119, `documentation/architecture/backend.md` line 428, `documentation/architecture/overview.md` line 79, `documentation/adr/0046-production-image-and-compose.md` point 6, and ADR 0042 point 26 (a new ADR explains the change, see 2.8). ADR 0042 point 26 already names `CONTRIBUTING.md` as a place to update.
5. The failure to avoid: an operator updates the code and does not add the second worker. Notification mail then waits in `jobs` and nothing says so. Proposals: (a) `SELF_HOSTING.md` says it in the first line of the update notes; (b) `php artisan app:health` (`app/Console/Commands/HealthCheckCommand.php`) gets a check `mail-queue`: it fails when jobs of the queue `mail` are older than 10 minutes (to check: whether `jobs.available_at` and `created_at` allow it with the database driver; yes for `database`, no for others, then the check says "not checked"). Since no mail uses the queue before 4c, the failure cannot happen before 4c.
6. `SELF_HOSTING.md`: the sentence at line 182 ("The worker reads three queues...") and the Supervisor example at lines 186 to 207 get the second program `knowii-queue-mail` with `--queue=mail --sleep=3`. The Docker section (line 325, "five services") becomes six. Do not write a count, only the names. The new values go in the table at line 76 ff.
7. A single-worker downgrade, for people who do not want two processes: `MAIL_QUEUE=default` (see 2.2 point 8). The mail then runs on the first worker, still behind the limiter.

### 2.5 The budget alert

1. What counts as a sent mail: one mail accepted by the transport, counted per recipient (to, cc, bcc), whatever the queue, the lane or the kind. The count is made in a listener of the Laravel event `Illuminate\Mail\Events\MessageSent` (`vendor/laravel/framework/src/Illuminate/Mail/Events/MessageSent.php`; it fires for mailables and for notifications, so it also counts the verification mail). A refused send (a 429) does not fire it, so it is not counted. A mail that Resend skips because the address is on its suppression list is accepted by the API, and Resend says it is not sent. To check whether it counts against the quota.
2. Where the count is kept: a small table `mail_usage_months` with `month` (the first day of a calendar month, UTC, unique), `sent_count`, `alert_80_sent_at`, `hold_notified_at`. The listener does one atomic `insert ... on conflict (month) do update set sent_count = sent_count + n` (PostgreSQL only, rule 10 of `documentation/conventions/rules.md`). It goes through an action behind a contract (rule 2), `RecordsMailSent`. The table holds no personal data: no retention period and no policy line (to confirm with the owner when the policy words are written). The cache is a bad place: `PruneExpiredData` clears the cache table, and a lost count means a missed alert.
3. Where the monthly quota comes from, in this order:
   1. `MAIL_MONTHLY_QUOTA` (a number). It wins. It is for any provider, and for a quota shared with other products.
   2. The provider, when `MAIL_MAILER=resend` and `RESEND_KEY` is set: `emails.monthly.limit` and `emails.monthly.used` from the usage endpoint. It is the real number and it includes the mail of other products on the same team, which a local counter cannot see. One call an hour is far below 10 requests a second.
   3. Nothing: no quota, no alert, no hold. This is the state of an instance with `log` or `smtp` and no `MAIL_MONTHLY_QUOTA`.
4. How it runs: a scheduled command `mail:check-budget`, every hour (`routes/console.php`, with `withoutOverlapping()->onOneServer()` like the other tasks, and a line in the list of scheduled tasks of `SELF_HOSTING.md` and a test like `RetentionTest` line 27). It reads the used and the limit as in point 3 (the provider number when the provider gives it, else the local count), and:
   1. At 80% or more: send the alert once for the month (`alert_80_sent_at`). It always writes `Log::warning`.
   2. At the hold level (point 7) or more: set the hold, and send one more alert once for the month.
5. Who gets the alert, and how: a new value `MAIL_ALERT_TO` (one address or a few, separated by commas). The repository has no "platform administrator" role (`config/communities.php` has only a list of addresses for reserved names), so the operator names the address. The alert is a plain mail (new mailable, level 0, on the `default` queue, not on `mail`) with the numbers, the month, the plan source and what happens next. It uses the same provider, which works until the quota is used. That is why the alert is at 80% and not at 99%. Without `MAIL_ALERT_TO` the alert is only the log line. The log (`LOG_STACK`) is the fallback that cannot fail for the same reason. See decision 2.
6. The text of the alert: plain, with the numbers ("This month the platform sent 40 100 of 50 000 emails (80%). Notification emails stop at 95%. Sign-in links and other account emails keep going.").
7. What happens above 100%: the provider refuses everything (429 `monthly_quota_exceeded`), the sign-in link included. That is the harm to avoid. Proposal: a hold at 95% of the quota (a config value `MAIL_NOTIFICATION_HOLD_PERCENT`, default 95; 100 means no reserve). Under a hold, notification mail jobs end without sending, with the delivery status `held`, and the notifications stay in the app and enter "About you" (design 6.5 point 4: "Nothing is lost"). Account mail (level 0) is never held and keeps trying. At 100% and over the account mail also fails at the provider; its jobs use the normal backoff of the mailables and a log line. The hold lifts at the start of the next month (`resets_at` when the provider gave it, else the first of the month UTC). The weekly budget of the design (7 per 7 days) is another rule and stays.
8. The daily quota of the Free plan (100 a day): the same command reads `emails.daily` when the provider gives it. A daily refusal holds notification mail until the next midnight UTC. Only matters if the owner stays on the Free plan, which the volume estimate does not allow (decision 1).
9. An instance without the provider (the `log` or `smtp` mailer): the limiter works with any mailer (value 0 turns it off). The counter works with any mailer. The check does nothing without a quota (point 3, case 3). No route or job needs the provider. The code path that calls the Resend usage endpoint runs only when the mailer is `resend` and the key is set, and its failure (network, plan) only logs and falls back to the local count.

## 3. #1478, slice 4b: bounces, complaints, the one-click stop

### 3.1 What the provider says

1. Event types (resend.com/docs/webhooks/event-types): `email.bounced`, `email.clicked`, `email.complained`, `email.delivered`, `email.delivery_delayed`, `email.failed`, `email.opened`, `email.received`, `email.scheduled`, `email.sent`, `email.suppressed`.
2. The payload of `email.bounced`: `type`, `created_at`, `data` with `email_id`, `message_id`, `from`, `to` (an array), `subject`, `tags` (an object), `broadcast_id`, `template_id`, and `data.bounce` with `type`, `subType`, `message`, `diagnosticCode`. The documented `bounce.type` values are `Permanent` and `Temporary`; the examples of `subType` are `Suppressed` and `MessageRejected`. Not confirmed: that these are the only values (to check: an `Undetermined` value, which Amazon SES has and Resend runs on). The docs show no field that returns our custom headers.
3. `email.complained` has the same `data` fields and no `bounce`. The docs say some providers (Gmail, Google Workspace) may not send complaint reports at all.
4. `email.suppressed`: Resend skipped the mail because the address is on the account-level suppression list. `data.suppressed` holds `type` and `message`; the example value is `OnAccountSuppressionList`. It has no `message_id`.
5. The signature (resend.com/docs/webhooks/verify-webhooks-requests, and the Svix documentation that it links, docs.svix.com/receiving/verifying-payloads/how-manual): three headers `svix-id`, `svix-timestamp` (Unix seconds) and `svix-signature`. The signed text is `svix-id + "." + svix-timestamp + "." + raw body`. The key is the secret without the `whsec_` prefix, decoded from base64. The algorithm is HMAC-SHA256, encoded in base64. The header is a list separated by spaces, each item `v1,<signature>`; any one may match. Compare in constant time. The raw body must be used. The id does not change on a resend of the same message. This is exactly what the vendored `Resend\WebhookSignature::verify($payload, $headers, $secret, $tolerance = 300)` does (`vendor/resend/resend-php/src/WebhookSignature.php`).
6. Retries (resend.com/docs/webhooks/retries-and-replays): exponential, at once, 5 s, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 5 h, 10 h, 10 h. An endpoint that keeps failing is disabled and the team gets an email. A message can be replayed by hand. Not confirmed: which response codes count as success, the timeout, whether events arrive in order, whether duplicates happen, whether the timestamp is renewed on each retry (if it is not, a late retry would fail a 5 minute tolerance), the IP addresses of the sender (not documented: do not rely on an IP list).
7. Resend keeps its own suppression list per team (resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/suppression-list). A permanent bounce and a complaint add the address. A mail to a listed address is skipped. An address can be removed in the dashboard or by API (`Suppression::remove()` in the SDK, up to 100 at a time). Resend warns that removal does not guarantee delivery and that the address is listed again if the cause remains.
8. One-click headers (resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/add-unsubscribe-to-transactional-emails): Resend says to add `List-Unsubscribe` (an https URL) and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` in the `headers` of the send call, to accept a POST at the same URL and answer a blank 200 or 202, to show the normal page on GET, and to stop within 48 hours. The Gmail and Yahoo rule on bulk senders starts above 5 000 mails a day to their domains; we are far below, but the design asks for the headers anyway. Not confirmed: whether the DKIM signature of Resend covers these two headers (RFC 8058 asks for it). To check with a test mail and the headers of the received message.

### 3.2 The webhook

1. Route: `POST /webhooks/resend` in `routes/web.php`, named `mail.webhook`, right under the OIDC one, with the same `withoutMiddleware` list (cookies, session, view errors, CSRF, Inertia) and `->middleware('throttle:mail-webhook')`. Controller: `app/Http/Controllers/Webhooks/MailWebhookController.php` with an action behind a contract (rule 2): `ReceivesMailWebhooks` bound to `ReceiveMailWebhook`. The name `resend` in the path is deliberate: the payload format is the one of Resend. A second provider would get its own route and its own receiver.
2. It is on only when `services.resend.webhook_secret` (from `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`) is set: otherwise 404, like the OIDC route. An instance with `log` or `smtp` has no route.
3. Signature check: call `Resend\WebhookSignature::verify()` with the raw body (`$request->getContent()`) and the three headers, tolerance 300 seconds. No new dependency (rule 11). One guard that the SDK does not have: refuse a secret that does not start with `whsec_` or whose base64 decoding is empty. The SDK's `getSecret()` returns an empty key for a bad value, and an HMAC with an empty key can be forged by anyone. Answer 404 (the route "is not configured") in that case and log it once. To check with a test.
4. Wrap the SDK call in our own class (`App\Support\MailWebhookSignature`) so a change of SDK does not change the route, and so a test can sign with a known secret. The OIDC test file has the pattern: build a valid call, then break one thing at a time.
5. Answers (same as the OIDC route): 401 with an empty body for any invalid call (no detail); 202 for a valid one, also for an event type we ignore and for a person we do not know. A 5xx would make Resend retry and then disable the endpoint. The code does the real work in a queued job on `default` (so the answer is fast), except the cheap idempotency insert.
6. Replay protection, two layers:
   1. The 300 second timestamp tolerance of the signature check. The server clock must be right (document NTP in `SELF_HOSTING.md`). Risk: a retry with an old timestamp is refused (to check, see 3.1 point 6). The replay button of the dashboard makes a new message, so the signed time is fresh.
   2. The `svix-id` is stored: table `mail_webhook_events` with `provider_event_id` (unique), `type`, `created_at`. The route does `insertOrIgnore`. If no row was added, the event was handled: answer 202 and do nothing. This also gives idempotency (point 8) and a trace of bounces (design 8.5: "to trace a bounce").
7. Rate limit: `RateLimiter::for('mail-webhook', ...)` per IP, `Limit::perMinute(Constants::MAIL_WEBHOOK_EVENTS_PER_MINUTE)`. The OIDC route uses 120 a minute. Mail events come in bursts (a 400 mail send at 08:00 gives 400 `delivered` events in a minute). So do not copy 120 blindly. Proposal: subscribe to only four events and set the limit to 600 a minute. If the limit is hit, Resend retries later (a 429 is a failure; retries are spread). To check: the real burst. Behind the proxy `$request->ip()` must be the real address (TrustProxies is in `AppServiceProvider`; to check that it is set in production).
8. Events read, and what each one does. Subscribe only to these four in the Resend dashboard (the others are noise, and the webhook must stay cheap):
   1. `email.bounced` with `bounce.type = Permanent`: a hard bounce (3.3).
   2. `email.bounced` with `bounce.type = Temporary`, or any value we do not know: a soft bounce. An unknown value is treated as soft and logged, because a wrong suppression is worse than a late one.
   3. `email.complained`: a complaint, treated as a hard bounce.
   4. `email.suppressed`: Resend refuses the address. We cannot send. Treat as a hard bounce with the reason `provider_list`, so the member sees why (3.3) and so that our own flag matches.
   5. `email.delivered`: only to reset the soft bounce counter (see 3.3). The design says "three soft bounces in a row", which needs the success event. Alternative without it: "three in 14 days". Decision 5.
9. Idempotency of the effect: a hard bounce sets a state that is already set (no change). A soft bounce adds 1, so the stored `svix-id` (point 6.2) is what stops a double count.
10. Linking an event to a mail (the main design problem). The payload has `email_id` and `to`. It has no custom header, and the Laravel transport sends no Resend tag, so we cannot add our own key to the mail at send time. The address alone is not enough: the same address can be an account and an invitation. So:
    1. A listener on `MessageSent` reads `X-Resend-Email-ID` (the transport adds it, see 1.10) and writes it on a row of the delivery log (3.5). The row says what kind of mail it was and for whom (a user, or an invitation).
    2. The webhook finds the row by `email_id`.
    3. No row found (a mail from before the feature, a race, a verification mail we did not tag): match by address on `users.email` only when no open invitation holds the same address. When both match, do nothing and log. A wrong suppression is worse than none.

### 3.3 The suppression model

1. Proposal: a table `email_suppressions`, one row per account at most: `user_id` (unique, cascade on delete, so account deletion removes it, ADR 0024), `email` (the address the row is about, lower case), `reason` (a backed enum `KnowiiEmailSuppressionReason`: `hard_bounce`, `complaint`, `soft_bounces`, `provider_list`; null while only soft bounces count), `suppressed_at` (null until suppressed), `soft_bounce_count` (small integer), `last_event_at`, timestamps, plus `cuid` (data rule 1: every table has `id`, `cuid` and timestamps).
2. The design (6.13 point 2) says `email_suppressed_at` on the account. A column on `users` works too (`email_suppressed_at`, `email_suppression_reason`, `email_soft_bounces`). The table is better for two reasons. First, it keeps three fields that every `User` read loads out of the model and out of the `User` API resource. Second, it holds the address that the row is about, which makes the lift on an address change automatic (next point). It is a deviation from the text of the design, so it is decision 4.
3. "An address change lifts it": a suppression is valid only while `email_suppressions.email` equals `users.email` (case-insensitive). One function `EmailSuppression::appliesTo(User $user)` is the only reader. A member who changes the address (`UpdateUserProfileInformation`, an OIDC takeover, or an operator) is no longer suppressed, with no hook in any of those paths. A hard bounce on the new address makes a new row for the new address (the old one is replaced). The mail that verifies the new address is level 0 and goes anyway.
4. A hard bounce, a complaint, and `email.suppressed` set `suppressed_at`, `reason` and `email`. From then, no mail of level 1 to 3 and no summary goes to the account. Level 0 mail (sign-in link, role, access, ownership) still goes (design 6.13 point 2), even though Resend will skip it when the address is on its list (3.1 point 7). The account then cannot get a sign-in link by email. A member can still sign in with a password or with the sign-in provider. This is a real risk: see section 6.
5. The soft bounce counter: each `Temporary` bounce adds 1 to `soft_bounce_count` for the same address (a row is created with `suppressed_at` null). At 3 the row becomes `soft_bounces`, suppressed. `email.delivered` for the same address sets the count to 0 (one cheap update, only when a row exists). So "in a row" means three bounces with no delivery in between. A hard bounce or complaint on an address that has a row replaces the reason.
6. The invitation address: a bounce linked (3.2 point 10) to an invitation mail never touches `email_suppressions`. It sets two columns on `community_invitations` (migration `2026_11_15_..._create_community_invitations` has `email`, `role`, timestamps only): `bounced_at` and `bounce_type` (`hard`, `soft`). The invitation resource shows it to the managers who see the invitation list ("This address could not be reached"), and an invite again (`InviteCommunityMember` replaces the invitation) resets it. The row goes with the invitation (30 days after expiry plus the 7 days of validity, `config/retention.php`).
7. How a member sees a wrong suppression: on the email settings page (#841, slice 4 rest) a banner "We could not deliver email to <address> (the address did not accept it / you marked an email as spam). Notification emails are paused. Account emails still try to reach you." Two buttons: "Try this address again" and "Change my address". The page is a Inertia page that reads one endpoint (next point). The wording is for the owner: this point gives the content, not the final words.
8. How a member lifts it: "Try this address again" deletes the row (or sets `suppressed_at` to null and the count to 0). If the next mail bounces, the webhook sets it again. Limit: once an hour per account (a rate limiter `mail-suppression`), so a button does not become a way to send many mails to a dead address. When the mailer is Resend and the address is on the Resend list, our lift is not enough: Resend skips the address until it is removed there. So the action also calls `suppressions->remove($email)` of the SDK, best effort, only for the `resend` mailer (to check: the exact error when the address is not on the list, and the permission of the API key: a sending-only key may not manage suppressions). A complaint can be lifted too, because the member asked for it; the page says it plainly.
9. Does the platform need its own suppression at all, if Resend already skips listed addresses? Yes: (a) instances with `smtp` or `ses` have no such list; (b) the member must see why and lift it; (c) the weekly budget and the ladder must not count a mail that will never go (design 6.2 point 5 lists "the address suppressed" as a stop at the last moment).
10. Data, per rules 8 and 10 of `documentation/conventions/rules.md`: `email_suppressions`, `mail_webhook_events` and the delivery log are new personal data (an address, a state, dates). See 3.8.

### 3.4 The stop route

1. Routes (all in `routes/web.php`, all with the same `withoutMiddleware` list as the webhook, but this one keeps `EncryptCookies`-free and session-free too; the signature is the only proof): `GET /mail/stop` named `mail.stop.show`, `POST /mail/stop` named `mail.stop.store`, `POST /mail/stop/undo` named `mail.stop.undo`. All three have the `signed` middleware (as `community-invitations.accept` does at `routes/web.php` line 63) and `throttle:mail-stop` per IP. A controller `App\Http\Controllers\Mail\MailStopController` calls an action `StopsEmailGroup` behind a contract.
2. How the link is signed: `URL::signedRoute('mail.stop.show', [...])`, with no expiry. The parameters: `user` (the cuid of the account) and `group` (an enum, see point 4). The signature is an HMAC over the whole URL with the application key. Permanent, because a mail stays in an inbox for years and a link that expires would break the promise of design 8.3 ("one click"). The cost: a change of `APP_KEY` makes old links invalid. Write it in the runbook. Alternative: a random token stored per account (`mail_stop_token`), which can be revoked. It adds a column and a lookup, and gives nothing the signature does not, for this threat. Recommend the signature (decision 7 asks only if the owner wants to be able to revoke).
3. The links of a mail: the footer link and the `List-Unsubscribe` header point to the same `mail.stop.show` URL (design 5.6 point 9: "They stop the same group as the footer link"). The header value is `<https://.../mail/stop?...&signature=...>`; `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. No `mailto:` form: the platform has no inbound mail address (decision 13 of the owner: no reply by email). To check: that a `https` only header is accepted by Gmail and Apple Mail (the RFC allows it).
4. What it stops: the group of the mail that carried it, as design 5.6 point 8 says: replies and mentions; direct messages; important news; the weekly summary; event emails. Not "one kind" (the kinds are an internal detail: a member does not know a `comment_reply` from a `mention`), and not "all notification mail" by the footer link (that is one of the choices on the page after the click: "Stop all email from <community>" and the pauses). The weekly summary is its own group, so the summary mail's link stops the summary only, and "Weekly summary only" in the footer of a notification mail is a different action (a settings change, which the signed link can also carry: `group=weekly_summary_only` is a mode, not a stop; to settle in slice 4c with the preferences).
5. The groups come from the legal basis table (decision 1 of the owner): the groups whose basis is "legitimate interest" can be stopped. Account mail has no group and no stop link. The care mail "can be stopped" (decision 1) and belongs to slice 6. So the enum starts with the five groups of 5.6 point 8 and the check by counsel may add or rename. The enum is the thing to build before the check is done (see decision 3).
6. Storage of the stop, a minimal table in 4b (the preference screens are #841, in a later PR): `notification_email_choices`, one row per account and group: `user_id` (cascade), `group`, `state` (a string: 4b writes only `off`; 4c adds `soon_then_together`, `twice_a_day` and so on, so do not make it a boolean), unique `(user_id, group)`. No row means the default of the group. The send check of the ladder (6.2 point 5, "the group switched off") reads this table. To check with the 4c design: whether the pause and the "stop all" use the same table (a proposal: the same table with `group = all` and a `until` column, but that is for slice 4c).
7. Why GET shows a page and only POST changes something: mail scanners, link checkers and some clients open every link in a mail (Microsoft Defender, Gmail previews, corporate gateways). A GET that changed the state would stop mail for every member behind such a gateway without their will. The design says it (6.13 point 5). RFC 8058 says the same: the mail client makes the POST itself. The page has one button "Stop <group name>", a form `method=post` to `mail.stop.store` with the signed URL as action.
8. The one-click POST from a mail client has no session and no CSRF token and a body `List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. It stops the group and answers 200 with an empty body (the guidance of Resend, 3.1 point 8). The form on the page sends the same POST with a field `source=page`. For that, the answer is the result page (point 10). Both reach the same action. The action is idempotent: stopping a group that is off changes nothing.
9. The rule "api is complete" (rule 4 of `rules.md`): the stop route is a public web route and not an API client call, like the OIDC webhook. The token client's own way to change email choices is the settings endpoints of #841 (see 3.7). Say so in the ADR.
10. The page after the click (design 5.6 point 8, 8.3 point 3, 9.10 point 7). Content, not final wording: a heading "You will not get <group name> emails any more." Then in text what stopped, and what still arrives ("You still get sign-in links, and emails about your account. You still get the other emails you chose."). Then a primary button "Undo", then (slice 4c, with the preferences) "Pause all email for 2 weeks", "Pause all email for 1 month", "Stop all email from <community>", and a link "Email settings" (after a sign-in). The page works with the keyboard and a screen reader, confirms in text (not in color or an icon only), has a real heading and a `lang`. It names no person and shows no address, because the viewer is not signed in and the link may be forwarded.
11. The undo: `POST /mail/stop/undo`, signed. The previous state is part of the signed URL (`restore=<state>`, computed when the result page is built), so no state is stored for the undo and a forwarded or old link cannot invent a state. In 4b the only previous state is "no row", so the undo deletes the row. To check in 4c: the restore of a richer state. After the undo the page says what is on again.
12. A bad signature: 403 with a plain page ("This link is not valid. Sign in and open your email settings."), no detail. An account that does not exist (deleted): the same answer as a success (202 or 200), so nobody can learn who has an account (same rule as the OIDC route).
13. Page technology: the page is shown without a session. Strict CSP (ADR 0007) means no inline style, so a Blade view that loads the built CSS is the simplest, or an Inertia page with `HandleInertiaRequests` kept. To check which one the build prefers: the OIDC route strips `HandleInertiaRequests`, but it renders no page. The brand tokens and both themes must work (definition of done). The proposal is an Inertia page in the guest layout; settle it in the first review.

### 3.5 The delivery log and the headers on every notification mail

1. Table `mail_deliveries` (the "delivery log" of design 6.14 point 8 and 8.5): `id`, `cuid`, `kind` (what the mail is: a group, or `invitation`, or `account`), `user_id` (nullable, cascade), `community_invitation_id` (nullable, cascade; at most one of the two set, with a check like ADR 0014), `notification_id` (nullable, cascade), `channel` (`email`), `status` (`sending`, `sent`, `failed`, `held`, `skipped`), `provider_email_id` (nullable, unique), `sent_at`, timestamps. Unique `(notification_id, channel)` where `notification_id` is not null (design 6.14 point 8). No text, no subject, no address (the address is on the account or the invitation). Kept 30 days.
2. The listener on `MessageSent` fills `provider_email_id` and `sent_at`. The mailable tells the listener which row it belongs to through an internal header `X-Knowii-Delivery: <cuid of the row>` (an opaque id, no personal data; it goes to the recipient, which is harmless but visible in the raw message; to check whether it can be removed after the send). For the three existing mails the base class is not used, so a small trait `HasDeliveryRow` adds the row and the header. This touches `InviteCommunityMember` (the invitation id) and is needed for the "bounce on an invitation never suppresses an account" rule. A mail without a row is the fall-back case in 3.2 point 10.3.
3. The headers on every notification mail are set in the base class `NotificationMail` (`headers()` of the Laravel 13 `Mailable`, or `withSymfonyMessage`): `List-Unsubscribe` with the signed URL of the group and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`. A test asserts them for every class that extends the base class (a loop over the classes), and asserts that the three level 0 mailables have none (design 5.6 point 9: "Account mail has no such header"). The Resend transport passes them (1.10).
4. The footer link of the mail body and the header use one builder, `MailStopLink::for($user, $group)`, so they cannot drift.

### 3.9 Tests for #1478

1. Webhook, signature: a valid call (signed with a known `whsec_` secret) gives 202; a wrong secret, a changed body, a missing header, an old timestamp (301 s), a future timestamp, a replayed `svix-id` each give 401 or 202 as designed; the same `svix-id` twice changes the state once; a secret without `whsec_` or with an empty decoding gives 404 and never verifies.
2. Webhook, public: no session, no cookie, no CSRF, no `auth`, no `ability`; limited per address (the pattern of the OIDC test at `EntitlementsProviderTest.php` line 561 and 579); 404 without a secret.
3. Events: a permanent bounce suppresses the account and sets the reason; a complaint does; `email.suppressed` does with `provider_list`; one temporary bounce does not; three temporary bounces do (`soft_bounces`); a `delivered` event in between resets the count; an unknown `bounce.type` counts as soft; an unknown event type gives 202 and nothing; a person we do not know gives the same 202.
4. Linking: an event for a mail row that belongs to an invitation sets `bounced_at` on the invitation and does not touch the account of the same address; an event for a mail row of a user suppresses the user; no row and an address that is both an account and an invitation changes nothing; no row and an address that is only an account suppresses it.
5. Address change lifts it: change the address, the account is not suppressed, a bounce on the new address makes a new row; changing it back does not revive the old state.
6. Level 0 mail is still sent to a suppressed account; level 1 to 3 mail is not (this belongs to 4c with the ladder; in 4b test the check function and the stub).
7. Member lift: the endpoint deletes the row; a second call within the hour gives 429; for the `resend` mailer the SDK `remove` is called (fake the contract) and a failure only logs; a token without `notifications:write` gets 403; another member's state is never readable.
8. ## 6. Risks and open points

1. A sign-in link can be delayed by a backlog of `notifications` jobs, because `default` is the last queue of the first worker (1.6, 2.2 point 6). Not new, but slice 4c adds big fan-outs. Option: a queue of its own for level 0 mail, or an earlier place in the list.
2. A member whose address is suppressed cannot get a sign-in link by email, because Resend skips the address (3.3 point 4). A member with a password or the sign-in provider is not hurt. A member with only the email link is locked out until they lift the suppression, which needs a sign-in. Possible fixes: a banner and a clear message on the sign-in page when the address is suppressed, or a lift route that checks the address by a code sent elsewhere. Not designed here. This needs a decision before 4c.
3. A complaint from Gmail or Google Workspace may never arrive (3.1 point 3), so a member who pressed "spam" may keep getting mail. The stop link in the mail is the answer, and the one-click header helps because Gmail shows "Unsubscribe" next to the sender.
4. The webhook can be disabled by Resend after too many failures (3.1 point 6), and then bounces stop arriving without a sign. A misconfigured secret does this (401 on every call). Add the line to `SELF_HOSTING.md` and to the runbook. A check in `mail:check-budget` of the last event time is possible and not designed.
5. The time of the server must be right for the 300 second tolerance (3.2 point 6). Resend's own tolerance and whether the timestamp is renewed on a retry are not confirmed.
6. Linking an event to a mail depends on `X-Resend-Email-ID` which the Laravel transport adds after the send (1.10). If a Laravel update changes it, the link breaks silently and falls back to the address rule. A test that sends through a faked Resend client and checks that the row gets the id guards it.
7. A crash between the send and the update of the claim row loses one notification mail (the at-most-once choice, 2.3.7). It is the right side to err on, and the notification stays in the app. It must be told to the owner.
8. The quota of Resend is per team (2.1 point 5). A local count cannot see the other products on the same team. The usage endpoint can, when the plan gives it (to check). Without it, set `MAIL_MONTHLY_QUOTA` lower than the plan.
9. The 429 text for quota refusals is not confirmed (2.3 point 9). The code that detects `daily_quota_exceeded` and `monthly_quota_exceeded` in the exception message must be tested with a real refusal message, or be written to also hold on any 429 that the usage reader confirms.
10. The health check of the `queue` service in `compose.production.yaml` finds a process by the name `queue:work` (2.4 point 4). It may call a dead mail worker healthy because the first worker has the same name. To check, and fix with the queue name in the check.
11. `HandleInertiaRequests` and the session are stripped on the stop routes. If the page is an Inertia page, shared props and the theme script need a check (3.4 point 13).
12. The weekly summary link ("Weekly summary only" in the footer of 5.6 point 7) is a different action from the stop and needs the preferences of slice 4c. 4b must not promise it (3.4 point 4).
13. The `email_suppressions` row for a soft bounce count creates a row for a person who never got suppressed. ## 7. Addition (2026-11-16): a suppressed address must never lock a member out

The owner read risk 2 of section 6 and said that the design must cover it. This section does. It changes 3.3 point 4 and 3.3 point 8, and it adds decision 12.

### 7.1 The problem

1. A hard bounce or a complaint puts the address on the list of the mail provider (3.1 point 7). The provider then skips every mail to that address, the mail that the person asks for included.
2. A member who signs in only with the email link then gets no link. They cannot sign in, so they cannot reach the page that lifts the suppression (3.3 point 8).
3. The sign-in page must not say that an address is suppressed: that would tell a stranger that the account exists and that its mail fails.

### 7.2 The rule: a mail that a person asks for is always tried

1. "Asked for" means that the person typed the address or pressed the button a moment ago: the sign-in link, the password reset, the mail that verifies an address, and the mail that confirms a change of address. An invitation is not in this list (a manager asks for it, not the person who gets it), and no notification mail is.
2. The suppression of the platform (`email_suppressions`) stops only the notification mail (levels 1 to 3 and the summary). It never stops a mail of point 1. This is what the design says (6.13 point 2); the new part is the next point.
3. Before the platform sends a mail of point 1 to an address that has a row in `email_suppressions` with a reason that also lists the address at the provider (`hard_bounce`, `complaint`, `provider_list`), it asks the provider to take the address off its list (`suppressions->remove()`), then it sends. Best effort: when the removal fails, the mail is sent anyway and the failure is logged.
4. A cap protects the reputation of the sender: at most 1 removal per address per hour and 3 per 24 hours (a named rate limiter, the values in the configuration). Above the cap the mail is still handed to the provider, which skips it.
5. The row of the platform stays as it is. Notification mail stays off until point 7.3 lifts it. So one asked-for mail goes out, and nothing else.
6. The sign-in page shows the same text in every case. It gets one more help line, for everyone: "No email after a few minutes? Look in your spam folder, then ask again. An address that refused our mail before can need a second try." It says nothing about an account.
7. When the mailer is not the provider with a list (`smtp`, `log`), points 3 and 4 do nothing.

### 7.3 How the suppression ends after that

1. Reason `hard_bounce`, `soft_bounces` or `provider_list`: when the provider reports `email.delivered` for the address, the address works. The platform deletes the row, and notification mail is on again. The bounce was about delivery, not about what the member wants.
2. Reason `complaint`: a delivery never lifts it. The member pressed "spam" on a notification mail, so only the member turns notification mail on again, with "Try this address again" on the settings page (3.3 point 8). The asked-for mail of 7.2 still reaches them.
3. If the asked-for mail bounces again, the provider lists the address again and the webhook keeps the row. The member can try again within the cap, or sign in another way.
4. After the sign-in, the banner of 3.3 point 7 tells the member what happened and offers "Try this address again" and "Change my address".

### 7.4 When the address is really dead

1. No mail can reach the member, so no design on the mail side helps. The ways that remain: a password, the sign-in provider, or the operator of the instance.
2. `SELF_HOSTING.md` and the runbook get a short part: how an operator checks the state of an address (`email_suppressions`, the list of the provider) and changes the address of an account after a check of the identity. A command for that change is to check (does one exist) and, if not, a small issue.
3. The alert mail to `MAIL_ALERT_TO` (2.5) gets one more case, off by default: an asked-for mail to a suppressed address reached the cap. It names the address to the operator only.

### 7.5 What can go wrong, and the answer

1. A stranger asks for sign-in links for the address of another person to force mail to an address that complained. Answer: the cap of 7.2 point 4, the rate limit that the sign-in route already has, and the fact that the mail is one sign-in link that the provider lists again on a new complaint.
2. The API key cannot remove an address from the list of the provider (a key for sending only: to check, see 3.3 point 8). Answer: the key of the instance needs that right for this rule to work. Without it the rule falls back to 7.4, and the `SELF_HOSTING.md` text says which right to give.
3. A removal succeeds but the mail still does not arrive (the provider warns about this, 3.1 point 7). Answer: nothing more to do on our side; 7.4 applies.

### 7.6 Tests

1. Each asked-for mail to an address with each reason: the removal is called once, then the mail is sent; the row stays.
2. The cap: the second request within the hour and the fourth within the day call no removal and still send.
3. A notification mail to the same address: not sent, no removal.
4. An invitation to the same address: no removal.
5. `email.delivered` deletes a `hard_bounce`, `soft_bounces` or `provider_list` row and keeps a `complaint` row.
6. The sign-in page answer is byte for byte the same for an unknown address, a healthy address and a suppressed address.
7. The `smtp` and `log` mailers: no call to the provider.

### 7.7 Where it goes in the split of section 5

PR 4 (the webhook and the suppression) gets 7.2, 7.3 and 7.6, because they need the table and the events. PR 6 keeps the member's view. The texts of 7.2 point 6 and of the banner go to the owner with the other words (decision 11).

### 7.8 Decision 12 for the owner

A complaint (the member pressed "spam") and an asked-for mail: recommendation, the asked-for mail still takes the address off the list of the provider and goes out, within the cap, because the person asks for it at that moment. Alternative: after a complaint no mail at all until an operator acts; safer for the reputation of the sender, but a member with only the email link is locked out.

## 8. What the read of the code changed for slice 4a (2026-11-17)

The brief of slice 4a was written against the code one day later. Where it differs from sections 1 and 2, this section is right.

1. The refusal for a quota (2.3 point 9): `failed()` runs after the job failed, and the transport wraps the error of the provider. So a job middleware (`HoldNotificationMail`) catches the transport exception, walks the previous exceptions, and reads the error type of the exception of the provider library (`monthly_quota_exceeded`, `daily_quota_exceeded`). It compares exact text, with no regular expression.
2. The same middleware is the hold (2.5 point 7): a held job ends as done, sends nothing, and does not go to `failed_jobs`. Its place is before the rate limiter.
3. The hold is kept on the row of the month (`mail_usage_months.held_until`), not in the cache: the check, the workers and the middleware read one place. A new month has a new row, so the hold lifts by itself. When the numbers are again below the hold level (the operator raised the quota), the check clears the hold.
4. The table `mail_usage_months` also gets a `cuid` (data rule 1).
5. The contract is `RecordsSentEmails` (the naming rules ask for a third-person verb and a plural noun), with the action `RecordSentEmails`. The listener never throws: an exception after a send would send the mail twice.
6. The health check of the production compose file (2.4 point 4, risk 10) is right as it is: each service is its own container, and the check reads the processes of its own container.
7. The daily quota (2.5 point 8): the hold for a day writes a log line and sends no alert mail, because that mail would use the daily quota.
8. `app:health` gets the check `mail-queue` (2.4 point 5): with the `database` driver it fails when a job of the mail queue waits for more than 10 minutes.
9. Slice 4a has nothing that a member can read or change: no route, no ability, no API document. The ADR says so.
10. The words of the alert mail (2.5 point 6) are approved by the owner (2026-11-17): the text at 80% as quoted, and a hold variant that says that notification emails are paused until the date and that account emails keep going.

## 9. What the build changed (slice 4b, 2026-11-23)

The build of slice 4b (#1478, [ADR 0054](../../adr/0054-email-delivery-the-mail-queue-the-budget-bounces-and-the-one-click-stop.md), "Slice 4b") differs from section 3 in these points. Where it differs, this section is right.
