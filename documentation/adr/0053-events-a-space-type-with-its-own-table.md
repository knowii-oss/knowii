# 0053. Events: a space type with its own table

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-18
- Issue: #1513 (slice E1a of #1388), #1514 (slice E1b: the screens, see "The screens" below), #1515 (slice E2a: the server of the answers and the places, see "The answers and the places" below; slice E2c: the calendar file and "My events", see "The calendar file and My events" below)
- Pull request: #1667 (the server part), #1684 (the screens)
- Amends: [0012](0012-space-groups-and-typed-spaces.md) (a new space type), [0042](0042-notifications.md) (point 34: "Everything" for events spaces, the kind `event_new`, `notifications.event_id`), [0044](0044-public-spaces-for-visitors.md) (point 9: three more public reads)

## Context

A community plans events: office hours, workshops, live sessions. The old tool keeps them in event spaces of their own. Knowii needs a dated item that members read in their own time zone, with a meeting address that only members see, and that tells the members once when it is published. The design of #1388 (2026-11-16, approved with its 18 decisions) compared three models:

1. A space type `events` with a table `events`. A space already carries visibility, access groups, posting policy, navigation, API and live updates; resources ([ADR 0015](0015-resources-space-type.md)) and links ([ADR 0026](0026-link-spaces.md)) joined the same way.
2. An event as a post with start and end columns in a discussion space. A post has no draft, no start, no answers, and its list is ordered by creation; Upcoming and Past would need a flag that is a space type under another name.
3. Events without a space. It rebuilds visibility, access groups, navigation and permissions: the duplicate that ADR 0015 removed.

## Decision

1. The model (design 3.1, option 1). A new case `events` of `KnowiiSpaceType`, and a table `events` (`App\Models\Event`; the folder `app/Events/` keeps the broadcast events). The type of a space never changes. The space form starts a new events space with the posting policy `moderators`. `SpacePolicy::contribute` (the posting policy) decides who creates an event; `CreateEvent` refuses another space type.
2. Times. `starts_at` and `ends_at` are UTC instants (`timestamptz`): the truth. `time_zone` is the IANA zone in which the host entered the time; it never changes the moment. The API takes ISO 8601 with an explicit offset and the zone, and checks that the offset is the real offset of the zone at that instant. A value without an offset, a wrong offset and a local time that the zone skips are field errors (400); the error of a skipped time names the last minute before and the first minute after the gap, from the zone data. A local time that exists twice is accepted with either real offset. The end is after the start and at most 24 hours later. A start in the past is for the owner and the admins only (to enter past events). PHP date classes and Carbon only. "Duplicate" keeps the wall-clock time of the zone, 7 days later, not 168 hours.
3. Past is derived. An event is past when `ends_at` is before now (`is_past` in the resource). No stored state, no job: a stored state needs a job to flip it, and to flip it back when a host moves the event.
4. States: `draft` (the host, the owner and the admins only), `published`, `cancelled` (still readable, so that a member with a link finds the news). Allowed: draft to published, published to draft (not once the event started: cancel it), published to cancelled, cancelled to published. `sequence` goes up with each change of state and each change of the start, the end, the address or the place.
5. One host (`host_id`, `nullOnDelete`). "Host" means the member named, while still a member and able to read the space. A host who left keeps `host_id` and loses every right; a deleted account leaves the event without a host. The owner, the admins and the host change an event; a moderator with `delete_content` who can read the space also cancels and deletes it when they can see it (never a draft). No new moderator right. "Duplicate" also needs the right to create an event in the space, goes through the time rules of a create (a skipped hour, a length that a change of the clocks would change, a start in the past), and gives the copy to the person who duplicates when the host may not host any more. The option of ADR 0043 that deletes the content of a member keeps the events they host.
6. `location_visibility`: `members` (every member who can see the event) or `going` (the host, the owner, the admins and, since #1515, the members who answered Yes). A visitor and a person who is not a member never get the meeting address or the place. The server cuts them in `EventResource` (`can_view_location`); the client never hides them. The server never opens the address.
7. Two token abilities ([ADR 0022](0022-api-token-abilities.md)): `events:read` (in the default set of a new token) and `events:write` (not).
8. Public reads (amends ADR 0044, point 9): `GET communities/{community}/events` (the upcoming events of the events spaces that the reader can read, with the guards of the public page), `GET .../spaces/{space}/events` and `GET .../spaces/{space}/events/{event}` accept a visitor, with `public-reads` and `events:read`. A draft is never in them for a reader who may not view it.
9. Live updates: `space.event.created` (at the publication from a draft), `space.event.updated` (a change of a published or cancelled event) and `space.event.deleted` (a delete, or a return to draft) on the space channel, through `SpaceContentChanged`, with `event_cuid` and `author_cuid` only. A draft sends nothing: only its host, the owner and the admins may know that it exists, and a person who may not see an event gets the answer of an event that does not exist (404). The read of an event answers 404 to a signed-in reader who cannot see it, as the writes do ("The answers and the places", point 3). The design named them `community.event.*`: the code sends the content of a space on its space channel.
10. Notifications (amends ADR 0042, point 34). An events space may be at "Everything", and "Everything" is its built-in default; a manager may set `all` or `mine` as its default. A new kind `event_new` (level 2, reason `space_everything`) is written once, at the first publication of an event whose start is in the future (`published_notified_at`), never for a draft, never inside `Notify::silenced()` (which still sets `published_notified_at`, so a later publication tells nobody); the age guard of the other items does not apply. The fan-out waits 5 minutes (`notify_now` skips it, for `SpacePolicy::moderate`) and reads the event again. Recipients: the members at "Everything" who can view the space and did not mute it, never the host, never a member who blocked the host. One row per member and event (`dedupe_key` `event_new:event:{id}`): these rows do not bundle. The item is a new column `notifications.event_id` (ADR 0014), with a check that exactly one of five items is set, and a partial unique index on `(user_id, kind, event_id)`. Back to draft deletes the rows of the event. The client writes the line in the zone of the reader: "Tue 18 Nov, 17:00 Central European Standard Time: title".
11. Uploads: `upload_usages.event_id` keeps the cover and the images of the description alive for `uploads:prune`; the check of `upload_usages` covers five items.

What waits for the later slices: the screens of the answers, the calendar file and "My events" (the rest of E2, #1515; built, see the parts below); comments, the replay link, the notes and "cancel it first" before a delete (E3, #1516); the kinds 26 to 29, the reminders and emails (E4 and E5, #1482); the summary block and the "Catch up" line (E6); the calendar feed (E7).

## The screens (#1514)

A change inside the same decision (slice E1b): the pages that read what slice E1a stores. No new API route, no change of `EventResource`, of the policies or of the actions.

1. Three web routes, all `GET`, served by `Inertia\EventController`, which only reads (every change goes through the API). `events.show`, `/community/{communitySlug}/space/{spaceSlug}/event/{eventSlug}`, is in the `auth.optional` group: a visitor reads an event of a public events space. `events.create`, `.../space/{spaceSlug}/events/new`, and `events.edit`, `.../event/{eventSlug}/edit`, are in the signed-in group. "events/new" is plural, so an event with the slug `new` never collides with it. The controller finds the space by slug and type, calls `NoAccess::authorizeView` and `TracksSpaceVisits`, then looks for the event: a reader who may not see it (a draft) gets 404, which is the sign-in page for a visitor (ADR 0044, point 4). A signed-in person who is not a member gets the same 404 from a draft of a public events space, not the page without access: a draft tells nobody that it exists. In a space that the reader may not read, the space is refused first (403). The form of a new event needs `SpacePolicy::contribute` (403), the form of an event needs `EventPolicy::update` (403).
2. Props. The event page: `event` with its description, and `permissions` (`canUpdate`, `canCancel`, `canDelete` from `EventPolicy`, `canDuplicate` from `update` and `SpacePolicy::contribute`, and since #1515 `canViewAnswers` from `EventPolicy::viewAnswers`), all false for a visitor and for a person who is not a member. The form: `event` (null for a new one), `timeZones` (`TimeZones::identifiers()`, so that the form offers only the zones that the server accepts) and `permissions` (`canNotifyNow` from `SpacePolicy::moderate`, `canManageAllEvents` for the owner and the admins). The page of an events space gets `events` (the first 20, `SpaceController::EVENTS_PER_PAGE`) and `eventsScope` (`upcoming` or `past`, from `?scope=`; any other value means `upcoming`). The home and the public page of a community get `nextEvents`: the first 3 (`Constants::NEXT_EVENTS`) of `App\Support\UpcomingEvents`, which also serves `GET communities/{community}/events`.
3. The order of "Upcoming" (design 6.1, points 3 and 5): the drafts first, then the next start first. `Event::scopeUpcoming` holds it, so the API (`GET .../spaces/{space}/events`) and the page of the space give the same order. Only the people who may see a draft get one, so the order of the other readers does not change. "Past" stays: the newest start first.
4. The time fields of the form (design 4.2 and 11.1): native `date` and `time` inputs, and the zone in the `Combobox` of the toolkit with the entries of `timeZoneOptions()` ("Brussels, Europe (UTC+1)", the long name of the zone under it), the zone of the account and the zone of the browser at the top. The form starts in the zone of the account, else of the browser, else UTC; an edit starts in the zone of the event. The client turns the local date and time into ISO 8601 with the offset of the zone (`zonedDateTimeToIso`, `Intl` only): a time that the zone skips shows the two times around the gap ("23:59 the day before" when the clocks go forward at midnight) and nothing is sent; a time that exists twice asks "summer time or winter time?", and the larger offset is the summer time. The server stays the last check. The zone names need no mapping: the browser and the list give the current name of a zone, and the server accepts an older name and stores the current one (#1678).
5. Every time on a screen is a `time` element with the UTC start in `datetime`, written in the zone of the account (else of the browser) with the zone in the words of `Intl` (`timeZoneName: 'long'`). The weekday comes from the date in that zone. A list shows the short form, with the long form for a screen reader; both name the year when it is not the current year. The event page adds "The host's time", a `time` element too, when the start reads differently on the clock of the zone of the event.
6. Live updates on the screens, never for a visitor: the list gets the one event that `space.event.*` names again, and inserts, replaces or removes its card (a 403 or a 404 removes it); the event page loads again, and the server decides what the reader then sees. `space.event.deleted` is no exception: "Back to draft" sends it, and the host, the owner and the admins keep the draft. The page of a draft also loads again on `space.event.created`, which its publication sends.
7. A visitor and a person who is not a member read, in a public events space, the list and the event page: the title, the cover, the description, the time and the host. The meeting address and the place are cut by `EventResource` on every screen (`tests/Feature/EventPagesTest.php` scans the four screens). An event page is never indexed: `Robots` does not list it.

Where the design is silent, the screens take these answers. The owner confirmed them on 2026-11-20, with the words as built and with a cancelled event kept in "Next events" under its label; the words are in one file, `Components/Events/event-words.ts`.

1. Words: "Load more" for the upcoming list (the design names only "Load older"); the four texts of the empty lists; "Draft. Only the host, the owner and the admins see it."; "The meeting address is for the host and the team." for a member of a `going` event (until #1515: the members now answer, and the sentence is "The meeting address is for the members who answer Yes.", see "The screens of the answers" below); the two questions before a cancel and a delete; "Publish now" with "Without it, members are told after 5 minutes."; "Draft saved", "Event published", "Changes saved"; "In your zone:" for both places where the design writes "In your zone:" and "For you:"; "Past events: 12" and "Upcoming events: 3" in the status after a press on "Past" or "Upcoming"; "Your changes are saved. The event is not published." and "The event is published. The host did not change." after the message of the server, when a later request of a publication fails.
2. An end on another day names that day ("Wednesday 18 November, 23:00 to Thursday 19 November, 01:00 ..."), and the year follows the month when it is not the current year.
3. The menu of the design ("Cancel event", "Back to draft", "Duplicate", "Delete") is on the event page, not in the form: a moderator cancels and deletes the event of another host, and cannot open its form.
4. The form had no field for `location_visibility`, for a limit of places and for a replay: until members could answer, `going` hid the address from every member. Since #1515 the form has the three fields ("The screens of the answers" below, point 9), sends them with a new event, and sends only the ones that changed with an edit.
5. "End date" shows only when the end time is not after the start time, set to the next day. It also shows for a stored event that ends on another day with an end time after its start time (24 hours over a change of the clocks), and then keeps its value until the person changes it.
6. The cover: a file field with a preview and "Remove the cover", and "Cover description" ("Leave empty when the image is decoration"). The upload route needs a description: the form sends the cover description, else "Cover of an event". `cover_alt` holds only what the host typed.
7. "Next events" is after the "Catch up" card and before "Recent Resources", and a cancelled event stays in it with the label "Cancelled" (the list of the API route holds cancelled events).

### The screens of the answers (#1515)

A change inside the same decision (slice E2b): the screens that read and write what slice E2a stores. No API field, no route, no policy and no action changes. The server part is one prop.

1. The prop. `EventController::show` adds `permissions.canViewAnswers`, the result of `EventPolicy::viewAnswers`: true for the owner, the admins and the host of a published or cancelled event, false for a moderator, a member, a person who is not a member, a visitor, and for every reader of a draft. The client cannot derive it: `capacity` is null for the host of an event without a limit, so no field of the event says who reads the answers. The form of the host needs no prop: every person who opens the form of a published or cancelled event passes `viewAnswers`, and reads the number of Yes answers from `metadata.counts` of the list.
2. The answer group (`Components/Events/EventAnswer.tsx`) is on the event page, after the host and before the meeting block. Its legend is "Your answer". It is a `fieldset` with a visible legend, and the radio group of `SegmentedControl` inside it takes the legend as its name; the fieldset itself has the role `presentation`, so that a screen reader says the name one time. The options are "Yes", "Replay only" (only with `replay_expected`, or when it is the answer of the member) and "No". Without an answer no radio is checked. It renders for a member while `can_answer` is true and the end of the event is not passed, and it sends no request when it renders: it reads `my_answer` of the event of the page.
3. The sentence "The host and the team see your answer (Yes or Replay only). Other members do not." is under the group at all times, as the description of the group: it does not come and go with the answer.
4. A save. The new choice shows at once, the group has `aria-busy` (never `disabled`: the focus stays), and a polite status says "Saving", then "Saved: Yes". A choice that changes while a save runs waits, and only the last one is sent after it: the arrow keys pass "Replay only" on the way from "Yes" to "No", so no choice is lost and none is sent two times. After a save the page loads the prop `event` again and no other prop (`router.reload({ only: ['event'] })`): the radio, the places and the meeting block then show what the server gave. The page runs one load at a time, because every load brings the event and two loads can land in the other order: a new load cancels the one that runs (the cancel function that Inertia gives in `onCancelToken`), a save of the answer cancels it too, and no load starts while a save runs. So an older event never lands after a newer one. A load of the whole page that was cancelled, or that was asked for while a save ran, is owed: the next load is of the whole page. While a save runs, an event that the page already got does not move the choice that is being saved; the answer of the server shows after the save. An answer that this page did not save (a second tab of the member) empties the status: "Saved: Yes" is never next to another answer. A save that ends after the member left the page calls nothing, and the last choice of the member is still sent.
5. A refusal checks the answer that the server holds again, shows the words of the server in an alert under the group (the message, or the error of the field `answer`), empties the status, and loads the prop `event` again. A 403 or a 404 loads the whole page again. "Clear my answer" is a button under the group for a member who has an answer: a radio cannot be unchecked, and "No" and "no answer" are two states. After the clear the status says "Answer cleared" and the focus is on the first radio. The alert of a refusal stays when the event that the page then loads takes the group away ("This event is cancelled. It takes no answers."): it shows under the heading "Your answer", with the old answer as text when one exists, and the heading (`tabIndex={-1}`) takes the focus, which was in the block. When only the radio with the focus goes away ("Replay only" after "This event has no replay."), the alert stays under the group and the checked radio takes the focus, or the first radio without an answer. The focus does not move when the member put it elsewhere after the refusal. The alert goes when the event takes answers again.
6. A full event (`places_left` 0) for a member whose answer is not Yes: the radio "Yes" has `aria-disabled` and names the places line "Full" as its description, and a click, Space and an arrow key on it send nothing and check nothing. A member whose answer is Yes keeps it checked. When that member chooses "Replay only", "No" or "Clear my answer", the page asks first in a `Dialog` (the decision of the owner of 2026-11-20: an answer saves at once, and another member can take the place before a correction): "Give up your place?", "This event is full. If you change your answer, another member can take your place.", with "Keep my place" (it has the focus; Escape and a click outside do the same: nothing is sent, and "Yes" is checked again with the focus) and "Give up my place" (the choice is then saved like every other choice), and an event that changes while the question is open saves nothing and does not close it.
7. A member reads their old answer as text ("Your answer: Yes") in place of the group when the event takes no answers any more: a cancelled event, an event that ended, and an event whose end passed on the open page. A visitor reads "Sign in to answer" on a published event that is not over. A person who is not a member, and every reader of a draft, get nothing; only the alert of point 5 shows for a member, after a refusal.
8. The places. With a limit, one line of plain text under the group: "28 places left", "1 place left", "Full", in the color of secondary text for every number. Without a limit, no line. No screen shows the limit to a member. The limit shows in two places only: the field of the form, and the line "12 of 40 places taken" of the dialog "Answers".
9. The meeting block shows only what it received. A member of a `going` event who gets no address reads "The meeting address is for the members who answer Yes." while the event takes answers, and "The meeting address is for the members who answered Yes." after that. A member who answers Yes reads "Join the meeting" with the event that the page loads after the save.
10. The labels (`EventAnswerBadges`), on a card of the list and next to the title in "Next events": "You said Yes" (with a check icon), "You said Replay only" and "You said No" on every event that the member answered, also a cancelled or past one, and "28 places left", "1 place left" or "Full" on a published event that is not over, always in the neutral variant. A visitor and a person who is not a member get none: the server gives them no answer and no places. No card and no line has an answer control.
11. "Answers" is a button next to "Edit" for `canViewAnswers`. It opens a `Dialog` ("The host, the owner and the admins see this list. Other members do not.") that loads the names when it opens, and each time it opens: 20 names of one answer (`EVENT_ANSWERS_PER_PAGE`), by name, each a link to the member page, with "Load more", the choice "Yes (12)" or "Replay only (3)" (only with `replay_expected` or a "Replay only" answer), "12 of 40 places taken" with a limit, and "4 members answered No." without a name. It has no export, no copy, no search and no time of an answer. The dialog does not listen to the live signal: the signal is sent only for the Yes answers of an event with a limit, so it cannot keep the list current. The list is current each time the dialog opens.
12. The form of the host gets three fields after "Place" and before "Host". "Who sees the meeting address and the place": "Every member" or "Members who answer Yes" (then "The host, the owner and the admins always see them."), and the hints of "Meeting address" and "Place" follow the choice. "Limit of places": digits in a text field, empty for no limit; another value shows "Use a whole number from 1 to 5000, or leave the field empty" and nothing is sent. "A replay will exist": a checkbox ("Members can then answer Replay only."). The form of a published or cancelled event reads the number of Yes answers one time (the list of the answers, with a limit of 1) and shows "12 members answered Yes." under the limit; with none, with a draft, with a new event and when the request fails it shows nothing. When the limit that the person typed is under that number, the line becomes "12 members answered Yes. Nobody is removed. No other member can answer Yes." in a polite status, when the person leaves the field: a hint, not an error, because the API accepts the value.
13. The live update. The event page also listens to `space.event.answers.changed`: for its event it loads the prop `event` again and no other prop, so the dialog "Answers", the focus, the status and the alert of the answer group stay. A signal that comes while the answer of the reader is saved starts no load: the load after the save is enough, and it is of the whole page when a signal asked for the whole page (point 4). The list of the space gets the event again and replaces its card. "Next events" and the form of the host listen to nothing. A visitor still opens no connection, and no request of the answers runs for a visitor.
14. The toolkit. No block is new. `SegmentedControl`: `value` accepts null (no radio checked), an option accepts `ariaDisabled` and `describedBy`, and the group accepts `busy`. In forced colors its chosen option has a border in the system color `Highlight`: the fill of the choice is lost there, and the radio is for screen readers only. `SaveStatus`: `savedText` in place of "Saved".

The words that the design does not give are in `event-words.ts`, marked `[W]`: proposals that wait for the owner, each one line to change. "Clear my answer", "Answer cleared", "1 place left", "Your answer: Yes" (and the two other answers); "You said Replay only", "You said No"; the two meeting sentences of point 9; the texts of the dialog of point 11 ("Answers to show" is the name of its choice, "Nobody answered Yes yet" and "Nobody answered Replay only yet" its empty states, "No member answered No." its line at zero); the texts of the form of point 12. The message of a wrong limit is with the other messages of the fields, in `event-request.schema.ts`. The sentence of point 3 uses the words of the approved policy sentence ("the host and the team") in place of "The host of this event" of the design (10.1 point 3), because the owner and the admins read the answers too.

## The answers and the places (#1515)

A change inside the same decision (slice E2a): the server part of what the decision listed for the later slices. A member answers an event through the API. No screen changes, no email is sent and no notification row is written: an answer tells nobody.

1. Who answers and who reads the answers. `EventPolicy::answer`: a member of the community for whom `view` passes, while the event is published and its end is now or later. The owner, the admins and the moderators answer like members; a visitor and a person who is not a member never answer. `EventPolicy::viewAnswers`: the owner, an admin or the host (`isHost`), and the event is not a draft. A moderator does not read the answers, also with the right `delete_content`: they are personal data that moderation does not need. Neither ability tests an access group: `view` does.
2. The meeting address of a `going` event. `viewLocation` also passes for a member whose answer to this event is `yes`. `replay` and `no` do not pass. The answer counts also when the event is cancelled or over.
3. The routes. `GET`, `PUT` and `DELETE .../events/{event}/answer` (the own answer, `events:read` and `events:write`) and `GET .../events/{event}/answers` (the list, with the new ability `event-answers:read`, never in the default set of a token; the policy still decides). Every one of them calls `EventPublication::assertVisible` first: a draft that the person cannot see and an event of a space that the person cannot read answer 404, with the body of an event that does not exist. Then 403 for a person who is not a member, then 422 for the state of the event, then 400 for a wrong field. The read of one event (`GET .../events/{event}`) takes the same path for every signed-in reader: a draft of another host, and a published or cancelled event of a space that the reader cannot read, answer 404 with that body. So every route of an event gives one answer to a person who cannot see it. A visitor keeps the answer of the public reads (401).
4. No answer of a member reaches another member. No response, no Inertia prop, no broadcast and no log line carries it. The three exceptions: the list of point 9 for the host, the owner and the admins, the own answer of the member, and the export of that member.
5. `EventResource` gets five fields. `my_answer` (`yes`, `replay`, `no` or null) is the answer of the reader. `can_answer` is the result of `answer`. `replay_expected` is for every reader. `places_left` is a number from 0 for a member who can see an event that has a limit, else null. `capacity`, the limit, is for a reader who passes `viewAnswers` or `update`, else null: with the places that are left it gives the number of Yes answers. For that reason a request with an API token also needs the ability of the list of the answers, `event-answers:read`: a token with `events:read` or `events:write` alone gets `places_left` as a member does and `capacity` null, also in the answer to its own change of the limit. A session does not change. A visitor and a person who is not a member get null, false, the boolean, null and null.
6. A list never runs a query per event for these fields. `App\Support\EventAnswers::forList` loads the answers of the reader for the page with one query and the Yes counts with one more (point 7 says which answers count), and reads the role of the reader once (`CommunityRoles::withKnownAdmins`). Tests compare the queries of a list of 2 events and of 20, for the API and for the Inertia pages, and the pages have a fixed ceiling.
7. An answer counts only while its member is a member of the community and can read the space of the event. The answer of a member who lost the access to the space stays in the table and counts for nothing: it is in no name list and in no count of the list, it takes no place (`places_left`, and the check "This event is full."), and it is not an answer for "nobody answered" of the return to draft. `EventAnswer::scopeOfReaders` is the one place of that rule: the reading rule of `SpacePolicy::view` for a member (the rule that the fan-out of `event_new` uses), written as one condition of the database, so that a count never loads rows. A test holds the two forms together for each visibility. When the access comes back, the answer counts again. A known edge: the event may then be over its limit. Nothing is deleted, `places_left` is 0, and no new Yes passes until enough places are free.
8. The log lines of the action hold the cuid of the event and never the answer, the name or the cuid of the member.
9. `GET .../answers` gives the members with one answer (`answer`: `yes`, the default, or `replay`) as `AuthorResource`, by name then by username, with `limit` and `offset` (ADR 0011). `metadata` holds the pagination and `counts` (`yes`, `replay`, `no`). No name for `no`, with any parameter, and no time of an answer. The database filters (point 7), counts, sorts and cuts the page: one query for the counts and one for the members of the page, whatever the number of answers.
10. The limit of places holds when two members answer at the same moment, with a lock and a constraint. `AnswerEvent` opens a transaction and reads the row of the event again with `lockForUpdate()`: every answer and every clear of one event waits there, so they run one after the other. Inside the lock it reads the state, the end and `capacity` from the fresh row, counts the `yes` rows that count (point 7), and refuses a new Yes (no row, or a row that is not `yes`) when the count is at or over the limit: 422, "This event is full." The same answer again changes nothing and is never refused for the limit. The write is one `upsert` on `(event_id, user_id)`, and the unique index stops a second row when the same member sends two answers at once. `UpdateEvent` (a change of `capacity`) and `SetEventState` take the same lock, so an answer never passes a cancel that runs at the same moment. `SetEventState` decides inside the lock, from the state and the start of the locked row and with the rights of the person asked again: the second of two changes of state sees what the first one wrote. Inside the lock `AnswerEvent` also reads the member again and runs every refusal again in its order, and it holds the row of the membership (`community_members`, a shared lock) until the commit: a leave that runs at the same moment waits and then deletes the answer with the others, or it ends first and the answer is refused. No row stays behind. A limit under the number of Yes answers is accepted: nobody is removed, and `places_left` is 0. A member who leaves a Yes frees a place, and nobody is promoted: there is no waiting list (#1413). The limit is a whole number in the request, from 1 to 5 000, or null: a JSON number without a fraction, or a text of digits as the other whole numbers of the API take it. `App\Rules\WholeNumber` checks it before `integer`, because `integer` alone takes the boolean `true` and stores 1; `true`, `false`, a fraction, a word and a list are a field error (400).
11. The states. A draft takes no answers, and its list of answers is closed. Published to draft is refused with 422, "Members answered this event. Cancel it instead.", when a member who can read the space has a row in `event_answers` (point 7): a "No" is a row too. The row of a member who lost the access stays with the draft, and counts again when the event is published and the access is back. Cancelled to published keeps the answers.
12. The live update. A new name `space.event.answers.changed` on the space channel, with `event_cuid` only: the cuid of the member who answered never goes on the channel. It is sent after the commit, only when the number of Yes answers of an event with a limit changed, so that an open page reads "places left" again. No other change of an answer sends anything. A change of `capacity`, of `replay_expected` or of `location_visibility` sends `space.event.updated`, as every change of a published event does. The design named it `community.event.answers.changed` and sent it at each answer.
13. The data rules. `event_answers` has `community_id`, `event_id` and `user_id`, all three with `cascadeOnDelete` and each with an index: a deleted event, account or community deletes the answers. `Community::removeUser` deletes the answers of the member in that community, and a member who comes back starts with none. An answer is deleted 90 days after the end of its event (`retention.event_answers_days`, the step `event_answers` of `retention:prune`, ADR 0030). The export of a member lists their answers (`event_answers` in `activity.json`: the event by cuid and title, the answer, the dates, no other person); the export of a community holds no answer. `DuplicateEvent` copies `capacity` and `replay_expected`, and the copy has no answers. The privacy policy tells about the answers, who sees them and how long they stay (section 2, item 9 and section 5, item 14, with the three sentences that the owner approved on 2026-11-17).

Where the design is silent, the server takes these answers. They are the recommendations of the build brief, built while the owner had not answered yet: each one is one rule to change.

1. `replay_expected` comes with this slice, not with slice E3: the answer "Replay only" needs it. `replay` is refused with a field error (400, "This event has no replay.") when it is false. A change to false keeps the `replay` rows: their members still read "Replay only" as their answer and choose Yes or No when they want, and the list of the host keeps them.
2. A cancelled event and an event that ended are frozen: no new answer, no change and no clear (422, "This event is cancelled. It takes no answers." and "This event is over. It takes no answers."), until the host publishes a cancelled event again. The old answer stays, and its member still reads it. A draft that the person can see answers 422, "This event is a draft. It takes no answers."
3. A member who loses the access to the space and stays in the community keeps their answer. No read shows it, it takes no place and is in no count while the access is away (point 7 above), it counts again when the access comes back, and the retention step deletes it. A delete could not be undone when a manager removes a group by mistake.
4. A member reads `places_left` and never the limit or the number of Yes answers. `capacity` is for the readers of the answers only.
5. The list of the answers carries `counts` in `metadata`, beside the pagination.
6. "Only when nobody answered" (design 3.4, point 5) means "no row of a member who can read the space": a "No" also blocks the return to draft.
7. The live update is narrower than the design: a person who listens to the channel does not learn the moment of each answer. The list of the host loads again when the host opens it.
8. The limiter `event-answers` allows 30 answers and clears a minute per member (`Constants::EVENT_ANSWERS_PER_MINUTE`).
9. The label of `event-answers:read` on the token screen: "Read the answers to the events that you host or manage".
10. A delete of a published future event removes its answers by cascade, and nothing tells a member: the rule "cancel it first" is slice E3 (#1516), and the lines for the members are slice E4 (#1482).

## The calendar file and My events (#1515)

A change inside the same decision (slice E2c, the last part of slice E2): a member downloads one calendar file per event ("Add to calendar"), and opens the page "My events". No migration: `events.sequence`, `events.slug` and the index `(user_id, answer)` of `event_answers` exist. No new dependency.

1. The route. `GET .../spaces/{space}/events/{event}/calendar-file` (`ApiRoutes::COMMUNITY_SPACE_EVENT_CALENDAR_FILE`), in the signed-in group with scoped bindings, with `events:read` and the limiter `calendar-files` (`Constants::CALENDAR_FILES_PER_MINUTE`, 30 per member). The refusals, in this order: 401 for a visitor, also in a public space; 404 by `EventPublication::assertVisible`, with the body of an event that does not exist; 403 for a signed-in person who is not a member ("Only the members of the community get the calendar file of an event"); 422 for a draft ("This event is a draft. It has no calendar file."). A draft gives no file to anybody, also to its host, the owner and the admins: the file has two states only (`CONFIRMED` and `CANCELLED`), a draft is not told to the members and its time is not final, and the routes of the answers refuse a draft in the same way. A published, a cancelled and an ended event give a file. Each refusal is the JSON error of the API, also for the plain link of a browser: the route middleware `json-refusals` (`AnswerRefusalsInJson`) runs before the end of an outdated session (`EndOutdatedSessions`, ADR 0059) and the sign-in check, and marks the request as one that wants JSON, so that a browser never saves a page or the sign-in page as the file. A session that a takeover ended gets the 401 of the API too.
2. The file (`App\Support\IcsFile`, RFC 5545): a final class with no state and no query that gets the values and returns the bytes; it knows nothing of a request, a user or a policy, and slice E5 attaches the same file to its mails. The content lines, in this order, each one time: `BEGIN:VCALENDAR`, `VERSION:2.0`, `PRODID:-//Knowii//Events//EN`, `BEGIN:VEVENT`, `UID` (the cuid of the event alone), `DTSTAMP`, `DTSTART` and `DTEND` in UTC (`20261118T160000Z`), `SUMMARY` (the title; "Cancelled: " and the title for a cancelled event), `DESCRIPTION` (point 4), `LOCATION` (point 5, absent without a value), `URL` (the absolute address of the event page), `SEQUENCE` (`events.sequence`), `STATUS` (`CONFIRMED` or `CANCELLED`), `END:VEVENT`, `END:VCALENDAR`. No other line: no `METHOD` (the answer stays in Knowii, so the content type has no `method` either), no `VTIMEZONE` and no `TZID` (UTC instants), no `VALARM`, no `ORGANIZER` and no `ATTENDEE` (they are for a group-scheduled event and need an email address), no `LAST-MODIFIED` (without `METHOD` it equals `DTSTAMP`), no `CREATED`, no `CATEGORIES`, no `X-` property. The text values are escaped (a backslash, a semicolon, a comma, each line break as `\n`; a tab becomes a space, other control characters go), so a title can never add a line or a property; the `URL` is a URI and gets no escape. Each line is at most 75 octets, folded with CRLF and one space, never inside a UTF-8 character; each line ends with CRLF, and the file has no byte order mark.
3. `DTSTAMP` is the later of `events.updated_at` and the `updated_at` of the answer of the reader to the event, when a row exists: on a `going` event a Yes changes what the file holds (the address), so it is a revision of the data of this file. It is never the time of the request: two downloads by the same reader of the same event, with the same answer and the same rights, give the same bytes. Its limits: it follows the answer of the reader, so it is not a stamp of the content of the file. It goes back when the answer that made it later goes away (a cleared answer: the stamp is again the one of the event), and it stays the same when the content changes without a write to the event or to the answer (a member who becomes an admin gets the address with the same `DTSTAMP` and the same `SEQUENCE`; a Yes cleared on a `going` event takes the address out with the same `SEQUENCE` and an older `DTSTAMP`). A first import is always right. A calendar that compares `SEQUENCE`, then `DTSTAMP`, before it takes a new copy of the same `UID` can ignore such a re-import: it then keeps the address of the earlier file (one that the reader could see at that download) or misses the new one. No file holds an address that the reader may not see at the moment of its download: each file is built for the rights of that moment. Each change of the event itself (a move, a cancel, a new title, place or meeting address) writes the event, so `DTSTAMP` goes up (and `SEQUENCE` too after a change of the times, of the meeting address or of the place, or of the state) and such a calendar takes the new copy.
4. `DESCRIPTION`: the first 300 characters of the description in plain text (`App\Support\Excerpt`), an empty line, "Event page: " and the address of the page, and "Meeting: " and the meeting address for a reader who may see it. With an empty description, the first part and the empty line are absent.
5. The meeting address and the place. The controller asks `viewLocation` one time and passes the meeting address and the place to `IcsFile` only when it passes; it never builds its own rule. `LOCATION` holds the place, else the meeting address. A reader who does not pass gets a file in which neither appears. Two readers with the same rights and the same state of their answers (no answer, or answers written at the same time) get the same bytes; when their answers have different times, their files differ only in `DTSTAMP` (point 3). A reader who sees the address and one who does not get files that differ only in `DESCRIPTION`, `LOCATION` and, by point 3, `DTSTAMP`.
6. What the file never holds: the name, the username, the email address or the cuid of a member, of the host or of the reader, the answer of the reader, the places that are left, the limit, the name of the community or of the space as a line, the full description. So the file of an event whose host deleted the account does not change.
7. The response: 200, `Content-Type: text/calendar; charset=utf-8`, `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="<slug>-<date>.ics"` (the slug of the event and the date of the start in the zone of the event, the same name for every reader; `event` when the slug is empty; only lower-case letters, digits and "-", so no `filename*`), `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Cache-Control: private, no-store` (the file differs per reader). The route writes no row, file or job of its own, no log line, and nothing that records which file a member asked for. What happens anyway, as on every route of the API with these middleware: the limiter `calendar-files` counts the requests of the member in the cache for one minute, a request with a token updates `last_used_at` of that token (Sanctum), and a session of the browser is written again at the end of the request (a row of `sessions` with `SESSION_DRIVER=database`). The file is built at each request, never signed, never given a public address, and never reachable without a session or a token of the member. A browser gets it only through the link of the page (a click, also "open in a new tab"): the request then sends the address of the page as the `Referer`, and only with it does Sanctum start the session of the browser on the API (`EnsureFrontendRequestsAreStateful`). A typed address, a pasted address or a bookmark sends no `Referer`, so it gets 401, also for a member who is signed in. A program uses a token.
8. "My events" (`App\Support\MyEvents`, `GET user/events` with `events:read`, and the page `/events`, `events.index`): the events to which the member answered `yes` or `replay`, in the state `published` or `cancelled` (never a draft, never `no` and never an event without an answer), and the events with `host_id` of the member, in every state (the own drafts are in it; a draft of another host never, also for the owner and the admins). For both ways the member is a member of the community of the event and can read its space today: never an event of a community that the member left (as a member or as the host), of a `groups` space that they cannot read any more, or of a public space of a community of which they are not a member; when the access comes back, the event is in the list again. An event that the member hosts and answered is one item. `answer` filters (`yes`, `replay`, `host`; another value: "Use yes, replay or host"), `scope` is `upcoming` or `past` with the scopes and the order of the list of a space, and `limit` and `offset` paginate (ADR 0011). The database filters, sorts and cuts the page: one query for the events, one for the total, and their hosts, covers, spaces (with the access groups that the policy reads) and communities for the whole page, then the answers of the reader and the Yes counts (`EventAnswers::preload`) and the roles of the member in all the communities of the page with one query (`CommunityRoles::withKnownAdminsIn`): a page of 5 and a page of 20 events of the same spaces, and a page with the events of 2 and of 6 communities, run the same number of queries. Each item goes through `EventResource`, so the list gives nothing that `GET .../events/{event}` does not give to the same reader.
9. The reading rule for the database exists one time: `App\Support\SpaceReaders::exists($spaceColumn, $user)` writes the reading rule of `SpacePolicy::view` for a member, with the user as a column of the outer query (`EventAnswer::scopeOfReaders`) or as one id (`Event::scopeReadableBy`). A test of each use holds it together with the policy, for the owner, an admin, a moderator, a member, a person who is not a member, and each visibility.
10. `EventResource` gives `community` (`cuid`, `slug`, `name`) when the relation is loaded, and `eventSchema` has it as an optional object. The lists set that relation for the policy, and the read of one event loads it, so they carry the field too: it holds only what a reader of the event already knows.
11. The page "My events" opens no live connection: it shows what the server gave when it loaded, and it sends one request per press on "Load more" or "Load older". A row is the card of the list of a space without its cover, with the space (and the community, for a member of several communities) above the title (the personal community of the account counts as one of them only when "My events" holds one of its events: it may hold an events space), the label "You host", and "Add to calendar" on every row that is not a draft. The user menu has the entry "My events" for every signed-in account; the main menu does not change.

Why no calendar feed in this slice (design 6.6, slice E7): it is a different privacy question. The file of this part is asked by the member, with a session or a token, one event at a time, and each request passes the access check of that moment. A feed is one secret address that works without a login: a new credential outside the tokens of ADR 0022, which gives the list of the events of a member, with the meeting addresses, to every program and person that holds the address, at each fetch, also after the member left a community unless each fetch checks the access again. It needs a reset, limits, a line in the policy and its own ADR.

Where the design is silent, this part takes the recommendations of its build brief; the owner said yes to each of them (2026-11-21), and the words wait for the owner with the pictures:

1. The `UID` is the cuid of the event alone, not the cuid and the host name of the instance (design 6.5 point 3): RFC 7986, section 5.3, says that a `UID` holds no host and no domain name. The cuid is unique and stable for the life of the event, and the address of the instance is in `URL`.
2. A cancelled event has "Cancelled: " before its title in `SUMMARY`, beside `STATUS:CANCELLED`: several calendar applications show nothing for the status of a file that a person imports by hand.
3. `LOCATION` holds the place, else the meeting address, and the meeting address is always in the description for a reader who may see it. The date in the name of the file is the date of the start in the zone of the event, so every member gets the same name.
4. "Upcoming" of "My events" has the own drafts first, then the next start first, as the list of a space: a member reads one order everywhere, and a draft is not lost under the published events.
5. The words marked [W] in `event-words.ts` ("You host", "Add to calendar: {title}", the sentence of the page, the context line "{community}, {space}", the two empty texts), the three words of the file (constants of `IcsFile`) and the three messages of the server.

Alternatives considered for this part: a library that writes iCalendar (a new dependency for 15 lines of output); `DTSTAMP` as the time of the request (the bytes then change at each download, and a calendar cannot tell a real revision); a `UID` with the host name of the instance (RFC 7986, section 5.3); a `VTIMEZONE` with a local time (more lines, and no gain for one instant); the `CONFERENCE` property of RFC 7986 for the meeting address (few applications read it); a method of `EventApiController` for the file (the file is the only answer of an event that is not JSON, with its own limiter and its own middleware: `EventCalendarFileApiController` keeps it apart).

## Consequences

1. Events get one navigation, one permission model, one settings screen and the same live updates as the other content of a community.
2. Every switch on the space type gets a case: the policies, the notification levels, the space form and the empty state of the space page.
3. A list of events across spaces reads the events spaces that the reader can read (`SpaceTree`, or the public events spaces), never a table of its own.
4. A time in the API always carries its offset, and the server is the last check of the zone data of its PHP. A government that changes a zone later does not move an event: the instant stays.
5. The privacy policy names events in the content of a member (section 2, item 4, approved on 2026-11-17).
6. The answers are personal data with a short life: every new read of an event passes through `EventResource`, which decides alone what a reader gets of them, and a new route of the answers starts with `EventPublication::assertVisible`.

## Alternatives considered

1. An event as a post with fields: no draft, no start, the wrong order, and a type under another name.
2. Events without a space: a second permission model and a second navigation.
3. A stored state "past": a job that flips it, and flips it back when an event moves.
4. A time without an offset, read in the zone of the event: an hour that the zone skips or repeats would be guessed by the server, where the host must decide.
5. Several hosts: not needed for the first version (decision 5 of the design).
6. A unique partial index in place of the lock, for the limit of places (#1515): an index stops a second row of one member, but it cannot count the rows of an event, so it cannot hold "at most N Yes answers". A counter column on the event would need the same lock to stay true.
7. A count of the answers for every member ("N are going", #1515): a count of people is pressure, and with a limit the places that are left already say what a member needs (design, decision 8).
