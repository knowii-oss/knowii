# 0061. Invitations carry access groups and tags, and many invitations come from a file

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Issue: #1390
- Pull request: #1714
- Amends: [0019](0019-community-invitations-by-email.md) (point 5: an import renews its invitation and does not replace it; the import stores who uploaded it; a manager replaces an invitation only when they may give its role; every invitation mail counts in the daily caps), [0054](0054-email-delivery-the-mail-queue-the-budget-bounces-and-the-one-click-stop.md) (point 1: the mail of an import goes on the mail queue, with the limiter and a hold; a single invitation stays on `default` and is refused past a daily cap and while the budget holds mail)
- Uses: [0060](0060-member-tags-labels-on-members.md) ("The contract for #1390 and #1391"), [0025](0025-access-groups.md), [0047](0047-entitlement-rules-feed-access-groups.md)

## Context

A community that moves to Knowii brings hundreds of people, often with a paid access group and labels (#1390). Until now a manager invites one address at a time, with a role (ADR 0019), and the person gets no access group and no tag at the join. Invite links (#1391) need the same grants at the join. A file of 1000 rows also means 1000 mails: the provider accepts 10 requests a second, and the sign-in links must never wait behind the invitations of a file (ADR 0054). The people of the file are not members: they gave nothing to the platform, and the invitation mail promises that their address is deleted 30 days after the invitation ends.

## Decision

### An invitation carries access groups and tags (part A)

1. Two link tables, `community_invitation_access_groups` and `community_invitation_member_tags`: the invitation, the group or the tag, `created_at`, a unique pair and an index on the second key. No `cuid`: no route and no answer names a row (data rule 1, as `member_tag_members`). Both keys cascade: a deleted group or tag leaves the invitation (the contract of ADR 0060, point 4), a deleted invitation takes its links.
2. `App\Support\InvitationGrants` is the one reader of the lists `access_groups` and `member_tags` (cuids of the same community, at most 50 and 20, a duplicate counts once; a list as it comes in holds at most twice as many items). The create reads the groups and the tags again in its transaction with a shared lock: one deleted meanwhile is left out. A manager replaces an invitation that has not expired only when they may give its role (as for a cancel); an invitation that expired gives nothing, and any manager replaces it. Groups need `manageAccessGroupMembers`, tags need `assignMemberTags` (the contract, point 3); each gate is checked by itself, so a later change of one rule holds. A personal API token also needs `groups:write` for groups and `member-tags:write` for tags (ADR 0060 decision 29). An empty list needs nothing. The refusals come in order: the fields (400), the role (403), a member (400), the lists (400 or 403), then the role of the invitation that the new one replaces (403). A replace checks only the role of the old invitation: the new invitation has the groups and the tags of the request, as after a cancel and a new invitation, which the same manager may do.
3. `App\Support\JoinGrants::apply($community, $member, $groups, $tags)` runs in the transaction of the join, after the attach, with the groups and the tags read in that transaction with a shared lock (a group or a tag deleted meanwhile is not given): groups through `AccessGroupMemberships::addManual` (by hand, `manual`), tags through `MemberTagAssignments::add` (the contract, points 1 and 5). A tag past the limit of 20 is skipped with a log line of cuids; any other failure fails the join. When a group was given, it sends `AccessGroupsChanged` and `SpaceTreeChanged` once, after the commit. A person who is a member already gets them and keeps their role.
4. An access group that an entitlement rule feeds can be put on an invitation: it names one person, as a hand add does, and the membership is by hand. Invite links decide their own rule (#1391).
5. The mail names no group and no tag. The signed link of the mail has no page: its `GET` joins and redirects to the community, with a banner that names no group and no tag. So a tag of the managers stays one after the join (ADR 0060 decision 14).
6. The list of the pending invitations is a page (`limit` 10 by default, 1000 at most, ADR 0011 consequence 3), each with its groups and its tags by name, read with one query each for the page. A reader of the invitations with `communities:read` sees the names of their groups and their tags (ADR 0060 decision 29: such a field follows the policy of the reader, with `communities:read`).
7. A personal community invites nobody, and an invitation of a personal community does not work.

### Many invitations come from a file (part B)

8. The format is CSV, UTF-8, with a header row: `email` (required), `name` (read and dropped), `role`, `access_groups`, `tags`; names in a cell are separated by `|` (a comma and a semicolon are the delimiters of spreadsheets). The delimiter is a comma, or a semicolon when the header holds none and holds a semicolon. Quotes follow RFC 4180 (a quote opens a quoted cell only as the first character of a cell, else it is a plain character; a quote still open at the end refuses the file), a lone CR ends a record too, a byte order mark is removed, any byte that is not UTF-8 refuses the file. Limits: 1000 rows, 1 MiB, 20 columns, 1000 characters per cell (`App\Constants`).
9. The server reads the file (`App\Support\InvitationImports\InvitationCsv`) from the temporary file of the request, one time. The file is never written to a disk, a queue, a cache or a log. The client never parses it.
10. Upload, preview, confirm, send, log. The preview is stored: an import in `preview` (`invitation_imports`) with one row per data row (`invitation_import_rows`), so the manager confirms what the server checked. A preview lives 24 hours; a new upload of the same person deletes their older preview.
11. The rows are checked with a fixed number of queries per file (the groups, the tags, the members, the join blocks and the pending invitations of these addresses, one query each). A row has one reason, the first of: `extra_cells`, `cell_too_long`, `invalid_email`, `duplicate_in_file`, `unknown_role`, `role_not_allowed`, `access_groups_not_allowed`, `unknown_access_group`, `ambiguous_access_group`, `tags_not_allowed`, `unknown_tag`, `too_many_tags`, `already_member`, `removed_by_manager`, `pending_invitation`. Addresses are compared in lower case. A group is found by its exact name, else by the one name equal without the case; a tag by its name without the case; never a new group or tag. An address of a person whom a manager removed is refused: an accepted invitation lifts the removal, and a file must not lift many removals by mistake. A valid pending invitation refuses its row; an expired one is replaced. Nothing else of an address shows.
12. The names of a row and the defaults of an import are jsonb lists, not link tables (data rule 2): a preview lives 24 hours and the confirm resolves every name again. The lasting links are those of the invitations, with their foreign keys.
13. Only the person who uploaded a preview confirms it: they saw it. In one transaction, with the import row then the community row locked, `for no key update` (JoinCommunity locks it `for update`: the two wait for each other), then the locks of the daily caps of decision 18: every valid row is checked again for the confirmer; then three checks refuse the whole import with nothing changed (the budget holds mail; the mails left before the hold of the month are fewer than the rows; the rows pass a daily cap of decision 18), with no number of the instance in the text; then each invitation is inserted with `on conflict do nothing`, so an invitation of the same address that came meanwhile refuses its row with no error.
14. The mail of a row goes through the job `SendInvitationImportMail` (the id of the row and its `queued_count`, the number of times that the row went to the queue), on the mail queue, behind `HoldInvitationImportMail` and the limiter `mail`, with the retries of `NotificationMail`. The hold works as the hold of the notification mail: while the budget holds mail, or when the provider refuses for a quota (read by the shared `MailQuotaRefusal`, which `HoldNotificationMail` uses too), the row is `held` and the job ends. The job skips a row whose invitation is gone, or whose address is a member now; else it counts the invitation from now (the link is valid 7 days from the mail) and sends the mail of a single invitation, in the name of the person who uploaded the file (else the owner). After the retries the row is `failed`. The job sends, holds, fails or skips only a row that is `queued` with its `queued_count`: the job of an earlier time of a row that went to the queue again changes nothing. When no row waits, the import is `done` (one conditional statement).
15. The log is the import: who uploaded it, the dates, and the outcome and the reason of each row (`valid`, `refused`, `queued`, `sent`, `held`, `failed`, `skipped`). The counts come from the rows, never from counters. "Send the mails that were not sent" queues the held and the failed rows again, at any time; "Invite again the people who did not join" queues the sent rows whose invitation still exists, one time, 3 days after the confirm at the earliest. Both need the role of every row and pass the three checks, with the same locks as the confirm. "Not sent" also holds a row that stayed `queued` longer than the retries of a mail (`Constants::MAIL_NOTIFICATION_RETRY_HOURS`, 2 hours), plus one attempt of its job (`Constants::MAIL_JOB_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`, 60 seconds, the timeout of the job), plus 15 minutes (`InvitationImports::staleQueuedBefore()`): no job of the row sends any more, so it no longer counts in the month either. A send again gives each row the next `queued_count`, so a job of the earlier time that a worker still runs changes nothing, and the new job sends the mail. The record of a sent import can be deleted early only when its mails went out: not while a row waits for its mail, and not before 24 hours after the last row went to the queue.
16. The routes need the new abilities `invitations:read` and `invitations:write`, not in the default set: the rows hold the addresses of people who are not members. The upload, the confirm and the send again are limited to 5 a minute per person. The live update `InvitationImportChanged` goes to the private channel of the uploader, with cuids only.
17. Privacy: the record of an import keeps the address of each row, the result and the reason, never the name. It lives as long as an invitation: `Retention::invitationImportsDays()` (the validity plus `expired_invitations_days`, 37 days today) after the confirm, and 24 hours for a preview that nobody sent (`retention.invitation_import_previews_hours`); the step `invitation_imports` of `retention:prune` deletes it. The community export lists the imports with their rows, the member export the rows with the address of the account, and an account deletion deletes them, compared without the case. No log line, broadcast, job payload or error text holds an address. The legal basis of the invitation mail is the legitimate interest of the community that invites (art. 6(1)(f)); its owner decides who is invited.

18. The daily caps of the invitation mails: an account and a community send at most 1000 invitation mails a day (`MAIL_INVITATIONS_PER_ACCOUNT_PER_DAY`, `MAIL_INVITATIONS_PER_COMMUNITY_PER_DAY`). Every invitation mail counts in both: a single invitation, a row of a confirm, a row of a send again, over the last 24 hours; the cap of an account covers all its communities, and the account is the one of the manager who sends. The count is the table `invitation_mails`: one row per mail that went to the queue (the community, the account, the time; both keys `nullOnDelete`; no `cuid` and no `updated_at`, as no route names a row and a row never changes), written in the transaction that queues the mail (`App\Support\InvitationMailCaps`). Not the rows of the imports: a manager deletes an import, and its rows would leave the count. Not the invitations: a cancel, an accept or a replace deletes them. The transaction first takes two advisory locks of the transaction, the community then the account (`pg_advisory_xact_lock` with a fixed namespace for each, always in this order): they serialize the requests that count the same caps, and no foreign key check, update or row lock of the `communities` and `users` rows waits for them, as `FOR UPDATE` on those rows would make every insert that refers to them wait until the commit of a confirm of 1000 rows. A single invitation takes them first, before it reads the invitations of the address. A single invitation stays immediate, never held (ADR 0054 point 1): it is refused past a cap (422) with the time from when it works again, and while the budget holds mail (422 with the end of the hold; no row of `invitation_mails`), so a single invitation waits for the end of a hold of the mail budget. A confirm and a send again are refused as a whole with how many can go now. The refusal reads both caps: the words of the cap that frees last, the fewer of the two numbers that can go now, and the later time. The prune step `invitation_mails` deletes a row that counts no more; the rows hold no address. A row names the account that sent the mail: the privacy policy names it (section 2, item 5, the placeholder `retention.invitation_mails`, 2 days at most), and the account export lists the rows of the account (the community and the time, `invitation_mails` in `activity.json`).
19. The month check of an import uses the quota of the budget: `MAIL_MONTHLY_QUOTA`, else the monthly limit of the provider that the hourly `mail:check-budget` read last (stored on the row of the month with the use that it read and `sent_count` at that time; the mail sent since then adds to that use). Without any known quota the month does not refuse, and the caps of decision 18 still apply.

## Consequences

1. An invitation by email and an invite link give their grants through the same two classes, `InvitationGrants` and `JoinGrants`.
2. A single invitation stays level 0 on `default`, never held (ADR 0054 point 1). The mail of a file goes on the mail queue: it waits behind the limiter and stops when the budget holds, and the log says so.
3. ADR 0019 point 5 holds for a single invitation. An import never replaces a valid invitation: it refuses its row, and it renews an expired one. An import stores who uploaded it; a single invitation still does not.
4. The import of another tool (#1396) calls `CreateInvitationImport::create()` with its own records, 1000 at a time. Limits that the operator sets per community (#1454) and bounces (#1478) are later work: until then the constants and the daily caps of decision 18 limit the harm of a file of dead addresses.

## Alternatives considered

1. A preview that the client keeps and sends again at the confirm: the client would hold the truth, and the file would be read twice.
2. A library for CSV: PHP reads RFC 4180 with a small reader (architecture rule 11), and a lone CR and the semicolon files need their own rules anyway.
3. The mail of an import on `default` with no limiter: 1000 mails go out at the speed of the worker, over the rate of the provider, and a sign-in link waits behind them; without a hold, a file can use the reserve that the sign-in links need.
4. A tag or a group created from an unknown name of a file: a typo would create a tag for hundreds of people (the recommendation of ADR 0060).
5. The name of a row stored to greet the person: the platform has no use for it before the person writes their own name.
6. A record kept longer than the invitation: the mail tells the person that the address is deleted that long after the invitation ends. The audit log (#934) is the place for a longer record without addresses.
7. The count of the daily caps from the rows of the imports or from the invitations: a delete of an import, a cancel, an accept or a replace would lower it. A counter per community and day: a day that starts at 00:00 lets twice the cap go around midnight, where the rows of the last 24 hours do not.
