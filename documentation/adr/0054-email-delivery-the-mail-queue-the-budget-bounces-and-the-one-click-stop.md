- Design: [email groundwork](../design/notifications/email-groundwork.md), sections 1, 2 and 8 (slice 4a), 3, 7 and 9 (slice 4b)
- Amended by: [0061](0061-invitations-carry-access-groups-and-tags-and-come-from-a-file.md) (point 1: the mail of an import of invitations from a file goes on the queue `mail`, with the limiter and a hold of its own; a single invitation is refused while the budget holds mail)

## Context

Slice 4c sends the first notification email. Before it, the platform needs a road for that mail that cannot hurt the rest:

1. The provider (Resend) accepts 10 requests a second per team, and refuses every mail over the monthly quota (and over the daily quota of the free plan), the sign-in link included. A burst of notification mail must not use the place of a sign-in link, and must not use the whole quota.
2. One worker reads `broadcasts,notifications,default` (ADR 0042 point 26). `smtp` has no timeout (`config/mail.php`): a slow send on that worker would stop the live updates.
3. A job that a rate limiter releases counts one attempt. With `--tries=3` in production, three releases would fail a mail that never failed.
4. Nobody knows when the quota runs out: there is no counter and no alert. The quota of the provider is per team, and each operator chooses whether other products send with the same team (owner, 2026-11-17).

## Decision

### Which mail goes where

1. Only notification mail goes on the queue `mail` (`Constants::QUEUE_MAIL`, the configuration key `mail.queue`, env `MAIL_QUEUE`, default `mail`). Code reads the configuration, never the constant. `MagicLink`, `CommunityInvitation`, `CommunityOwnershipTransferred` and the address verification stay as they are, on `default`, with no limiter and no hold: they are level 0 and never held (design 6.13 point 6). So are the two mails of the sign-up, `FinishSignUp` and `SignUpAccountExists` ([ADR 0065](0065-the-sign-up-makes-the-account-when-the-link-of-its-mail-is-opened.md)), and the mail of a change to an address that another account has, `EmailAddressHasAccount`, with its job `SendEmailChangeMail` ([ADR 0058](0058-a-change-of-the-email-address-applies-after-its-confirmation.md)).
2. `MAIL_QUEUE=default` is the documented downgrade for an instance with one worker: the limiter and the hold still apply.
3. Every notification mail extends the abstract `App\Mail\NotificationMail` (`Mailable`, `ShouldQueue`). It fixes the queue (set when the mail is queued, so a subclass needs no code of its own), the middleware `[HoldNotificationMail, RateLimited('mail')]` in this order (slice 4b makes it `[HideMailErrorText, HoldNotificationMail, SkipSuppressedNotificationMail, RateLimited('mail')]`, point 25), `retryUntil()` (2 hours after the mail is due, `Constants::MAIL_NOTIFICATION_RETRY_HOURS`: the time it was queued, or the end of the delay of `later()`), `$maxExceptions = 3` and `backoff()` `[60, 300, 1800]`. With `retryUntil`, the worker ignores `--tries`, so in these 2 hours a release never fails a job; only real exceptions count. After them, the worker fails the job the next time it takes it (`Worker::markJobAsFailedIfAlreadyExceedsMaxAttempts`), also a job that never ran: a mail that waited for too long (a stopped mail worker, a backlog of more than 2 hours behind the limit) is not sent late. It goes to `failed_jobs` and the notification stays in the app. The backoff is read by the number of the attempt, and a release counts as an attempt: a mail with no release waits 1 minute after its first exception and 5 minutes after the second one, and the third one fails it; a mail that was released before waits longer. No subclass ships before slice 4c.

### The rate limit

4. `RateLimiter::for('mail')` in `AppServiceProvider`: `Limit::perSecond(MAIL_RATE_LIMIT_PER_SECOND)->by('mail')`, default 5 (half of the provider limit, so that the level 0 mail on `default` keeps room), `Limit::none()` for 0, and a value below 0 counts as 0. One global key: two mail workers share the count. The vendored middleware releases a limited job for the time left plus 3 seconds; a release is not a failure. The limit is not exact with two mail workers: the middleware reads the count and then adds to it in two steps, and the `database` cache store counts in whole seconds, so a second can carry up to twice the value. The default of 5 keeps that below the 10 of the provider.

### The hold and the quota refusal

5. `App\Jobs\Middleware\HoldNotificationMail` comes first. While notification mail is held, it logs one info line and ends the job as done: nothing is sent, nothing goes to `failed_jobs`, the notification stays in the app (design 6.5 point 4).
6. Else it runs the job and catches the transport exception. The design said "detect the codes in the message, in `failed()`". The code does not allow it: `failed()` runs after the job failed, and the transport wraps the error of the provider. So the middleware walks the previous exceptions to the `Resend\Exceptions\ErrorException` and compares its error type, exact text: `monthly_quota_exceeded` holds notification mail until the first day of the next month, `daily_quota_exceeded` until the next 00:00 UTC, with a warning, and the job ends. Any other error is thrown again, for the backoff.

### The count of sent mail

7. Table `mail_usage_months`: `id`, `cuid`, `month` (the first day of a calendar month in UTC, unique), `sent_count`, `alert_80_sent_at`, `hold_notified_at`, `held_until`, timestamps. No personal data: no retention period, no line in the privacy policy, nothing in the member export.
8. The listener `App\Listeners\CountSentEmails` (`MessageSent`) counts the recipients of the envelope (to, cc and bcc), whatever the mailer, the queue or the kind of mail, through `App\Contracts\Mail\RecordsSentEmails` (`RecordSentEmails`): one statement, `insert ... on conflict (month) do update set sent_count = mail_usage_months.sent_count + excluded.sent_count`. A refused send fires no event, so it counts nothing. The listener never throws: an exception after a send would retry the job and send the mail twice. The design named the contract `RecordsMailSent`; the naming rules ask for a third-person verb and a plural noun.

### The budget check

9. `mail:check-budget` runs every hour (`withoutOverlapping()->onOneServer()`) and calls `App\Contracts\Mail\ChecksMailBudgets` (`CheckMailBudget`: `check()`, `isHeld()`, `holdUntil()`). All the state is on the row of the current month: a new month has a new row, and the hold lifts by itself. The design kept the hold in the cache; the row is one place for the check, the workers and the middleware, and a cleared cache (`php artisan cache:clear`) does not lift the hold. The lock of the scheduled run ends after 10 minutes, so a killed run never stops the check for a day.
10. The quota: `MAIL_MONTHLY_QUOTA`, else the monthly limit of the provider, else none, and the check does nothing more (an instance with `log` or `smtp`). The use: the monthly count of the provider when the read worked (it sees the other senders of the same team), else `sent_count`. The quota is configuration (owner decision 1); the read of the provider is an option.
11. The read: `App\Contracts\Mail\ReadsMailUsage` (`ResendMailUsage`), only with the mailer `resend` and a key. It calls the usage endpoint of the SDK with a client of 10 seconds of timeout (to connect, and in total) and narrows the answer. Any failure gives null and one warning with the error type of the provider (`restricted_api_key` for a key that cannot read the usage), never the key or the message.
12. At 80% (`Constants::MAIL_BUDGET_ALERT_PERCENT`), once a month: a warning with the numbers, and the alert mail `App\Mail\MailBudgetAlert` to each valid address of `MAIL_ALERT_TO` (an invalid address is skipped). Without an address, the log line is the alert. The address is configuration, never in the code (owner decision 2): anyone can host the platform.
13. At `MAIL_NOTIFICATION_HOLD_PERCENT` (default 95, kept between 1 and 100; 100 means no reserve): `held_until` is the monthly reset time of the provider, else the first day of the next month, with a second warning and alert once a month (one mail when both steps come in the same run). When the provider gives a daily limit and the day reaches the hold percent of it, `held_until` is at least the daily reset time (else the next 00:00 UTC), with a log line and no mail: a mail would use the daily quota. When both numbers are below the hold percent, the check clears `held_until` (the operator raised the quota). So a hold set by a refusal can end at the next check when the numbers of the provider disagree; the next refusal sets it again. The check does not clear a hold when the read of the provider failed: the count of this instance can be lower than the count of the team, and one timeout must not lift the hold. A hold that comes back in the same month is a warning in the log, with no second mail. `holdUntil()` writes in one statement, so a shorter hold of another worker never replaces a longer one.
14. `MailBudgetAlert` is level 0: a plain mailable on `default`, never held. Its words are the ones that the owner approved on 2026-11-17.

### The second worker

15. Each file that runs a worker runs a second one that reads only `mail`, with the flags of the first worker of the same file: `queue_worker_mail` in `docker/8.5/supervisord.conf`, the service `queue-mail` in `compose.production.yaml`, `knowii-queue-mail` in `SELF_HOSTING.md`, a second `queue:listen` in `CONTRIBUTING.md`. The first worker keeps `--queue=broadcasts,notifications,default` and never reads `mail`. A test checks the four files.
16. The service `queue-mail` keeps the health check `knowii-healthcheck-process queue:work`. The design feared that it calls a dead mail worker healthy; it cannot, because each service is its own container and the script reads the processes of its own container.
17. `app:health` gets the check `mail-queue`: with the `database` queue it fails when a job of the mail queue is not reserved and could run for more than 10 minutes (`Constants::MAIL_QUEUE_STALE_MINUTES`), and no job of that queue ended in the same time. A released job waits for a later time and does not count. The listener `App\Listeners\RecordMailWorkerActivity` (`JobProcessed`) notes each ended job of the mail queue in the cache (sent, held or released): with one worker and a long backlog, old jobs wait while the worker runs, and the deployment workflow rolls back when `app:health` fails. A send that hangs ends no job, so it does not hide a blocked worker. With another driver the check passes.

### API parity

18. Slice 4a has nothing that a member can read or change (slice 4b adds the email status, point 31). The operator reads the numbers with `mail:check-budget`, the log and the alert mail, and changes them only through configuration. So there is no route, no token ability, no Bruno request and no change of the OpenAPI document.

### Slice 4b: bounces, complaints and the one-click stop (#1478)

Slice 4b tells the platform when a mail does not arrive, and lets a member stop a group of notification email in one click. It sends no notification email: the stop route and the headers are merged dark, and slice 4c sends the first mail that carries them.

The webhook:

19. `POST /webhooks/resend` (`mail.webhook`), under the change call of the sign-in provider and with its `withoutMiddleware` list (no cookie, no session, no CSRF token, no Inertia), limited per IP address (`mail-webhook`, `Constants::MAIL_WEBHOOK_EVENTS_PER_MINUTE`, 600). `MailWebhookController` calls `App\Contracts\Mail\ReceivesMailWebhooks` (`ReceiveMailWebhook`). It answers 404 when the instance has no usable secret (`services.resend.webhook_secret`, `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`), 413 with an empty body for a body over 256 KB (`Constants::MAIL_WEBHOOK_MAX_BODY_BYTES`, before any hash), 401 with an empty body for any call that is not valid (a missing or malformed header, a signature or a time that is not valid), and 202 for a valid call, also for an event that was handled, an event type that the platform ignores and a person that it does not know.
20. The signature (Svix, the Standard Webhooks scheme) is checked by `App\Support\MailWebhookSignature`, not by the verifier of the provider library: the secret is `whsec_` and strict base64 that decodes to a value that is not empty, else the route is off (one warning a day); the id is a short token, the time a whole number of seconds; any `v1` item may match, compared in constant time; the time is at most `Constants::MAIL_WEBHOOK_TOLERANCE_SECONDS` (300) away from the clock of the server. The raw body is signed. A test checks that no code of the application calls the verifier of the library.
21. After a valid check, and only then, the id (`svix-id`, the same on every retry) goes to `mail_webhook_events` with `insert ... on conflict do nothing`, in one transaction with the push of the job (the queue is the database): an id that is there was handled, a refused call never uses up the id of a real event, and a failed push keeps no id, so the retry of the provider handles the event. The work runs in the job `HandleMailWebhookEvent` on `default`, encrypted (it holds an address) and behind `HideMailErrorText`. The log holds the event type and cuids, never an address, a header, the secret or the body.
22. The events: `email.bounced` `Permanent` is a hard bounce, `Transient`, `Undetermined` and `Temporary` a soft bounce, and any other or missing type a soft bounce with a log line (a wrong suppression is worse than a late one); `email.complained` a complaint; `email.suppressed` the reason `provider_list`, handled as a hard bounce; `email.delivered` resets the count of soft bounces and lifts a suppression. Any other type, or a payload without `data.email_id` or without exactly one address, does nothing.

The delivery log:

23. `mail_deliveries`: `kind` (`KnowiiMailKind`: the five groups, `invitation`, `account`, `asked_for`, `operator`), `user_id` (`cascadeOnDelete`) or `community_invitation_id` (`nullOnDelete`, so a late report on a deleted invitation still reads `kind = invitation`), at most one of them, `notification_id` (unique with `channel`), `status`, `provider_email_id` (unique), `sent_at`. No address, no subject, no text. `App\Support\MailKinds` is the one map from a mail class to its kind; a test fails for a class of `app/Mail` or `app/Notifications` that it does not name. The address check and the password reset get no row; every other mail gets one (`App\Mail\Concerns\HasDeliveryRow`, written by the sender before the queue) and the header `X-Knowii-Delivery` with its cuid.
24. `App\Listeners\RecordMailDelivery` (`MessageSent`) writes `sent`, `sent_at` and the id of the provider from the header `X-Resend-Email-ID` of the Resend transport, never from the message id (the other transports give their own). It never throws.
25. A notification mail goes out at most once (consequence 4): the row is `queued` before the queue (one per notification); `NotificationMail::send()` claims it with one conditional update (`queued` or `failed` to `sending`) and sends nothing when nothing changed; a transport error sets `failed`, the hold `held`, and `SkipSuppressedNotificationMail` (after the hold, before the limiter) `skipped` when the address is suppressed or the member stopped the group. `HideMailErrorText` (#1730) comes first: a failed send keeps no text of the error, which can name the recipient, and the hold still reads a refusal for a quota inside it.
26. A report finds its mail by `data.email_id`: an invitation changes only the invitation (`bounced_at`, `bounce_type` `hard` or `soft`; a new send of a row of an import clears them), the alert of the operator nothing, a mail of an account the account when the report is about the address of the account. Without a row, the report counts only when `data.from` is the sender address of the instance (`mail.from.address`: the webhook belongs to the whole team at the provider, which other products can share), and the address (with or without a display name) finds an account only when no invitation holds the same address.

The suppression:

27. `email_suppressions`, one row per account about one address (`reason`: `hard_bounce`, `complaint`, `soft_bounces`, `provider_list`). `App\Support\EmailSuppression` is the only reader: a row applies while it is suppressed and its address is the address of the account, and a model event of `User` deletes the rows of the address of before when the address changes. 3 soft bounces in a row suppress (`Constants::EMAIL_SOFT_BOUNCES_TO_SUPPRESS`); a delivery puts the count back to 0 and deletes a suppression that is not a complaint, unless it is older than the last report. A complaint is lifted only by the member.
28. A mail that a person asks for (`asked_for`: the sign-in link, the password reset, the address check, the confirmation of a new address) is always handed to the mailer. Before it, `App\Listeners\TakeAddressOffProviderList` (`MessageSending`) asks Resend to take the address off its list (`App\Contracts\Mail\RemovesProviderSuppressions`, `RemoveResendSuppression`, 10 seconds of timeout), only with the mailer `resend` and a reason that the provider lists, within the cap `mail-provider-removals` (`MAIL_PROVIDER_REMOVALS_PER_HOUR` 1, `MAIL_PROVIDER_REMOVALS_PER_DAY` 3; the check and the count are one step under a lock of the address), with the address encoded in the path of the call. The row stays. The sign-in form gives the same answer for every address, with one more help line. Account mail and invitations go as before, with no removal.

The stop:

29. `App\Support\MailStopLink::for()` builds a permanent signed address of `mail.stop.show` (the cuid of the account and the group, `URL::signedRoute`, no expiry, owner decision 7) for the `List-Unsubscribe` header and the footer link of a notification mail, when the mail is sent. `GET /mail/stop` shows the page and changes nothing; `POST /mail/stop` stops the group (the one-click POST of RFC 8058 answers 200 with an empty body; the POST of the page, `source=page`, a 303 to the page); `POST /mail/stop/undo` restores the signed `restore` (in 4b `default`: the row goes). All three have `throttle:mail-stop` (60 a minute per IP address, never per account) before `signed`, and no sign-in. A bad signature gives the stop page in its "not valid" state (GET) or an empty 403 (POST). A link of an account that does not exist gives the answers of a live account and writes nothing. The address is a secret address (ADR 0064).
30. `KnowiiNotificationEmailGroup` (`replies_and_mentions`, `direct_messages`, `important_news`, `weekly_summary`, `events`) and `notification_email_choices` (one row per account and group; no row is the default; 4b writes only `off`, through `App\Contracts\Notifications\StopsEmailGroups`, which sets `off` whatever state the row held). The stop counts for every community. Account mail has no header and no stop link. The footer (`emails.partials.notification-footer`) lists "Stop emails like this" and "Settings", then the operator and `LEGAL_POSTAL_ADDRESS`. The routes of the webhook and of the stop have no token ability: `ApiTokenAbilitiesTest` lists them by name. The stop is a public web route like the webhook; a token client changes the choices with the settings of #841 later, and reads them now.
31. API parity: `GET /api/v1/notifications/email-status` (`notifications:read`: `suppressed`, `reason`, `since`, `address_hint`, `email_choices`) and `DELETE /api/v1/notifications/email-suppression` (`notifications:write`, "Try this address again", once an hour per account, `mail-suppression`, with the removal at the provider within the same cap). The notification settings page uses them.
32. Retention: the delivery log and the ids of the reports 30 days (`RETENTION_MAIL_DELIVERIES_DAYS`, `RETENTION_MAIL_WEBHOOK_EVENTS_DAYS`), a count of soft bounces that stopped nothing 90 days after the last one (`RETENTION_EMAIL_SOFT_BOUNCES_DAYS`), a row of another address at the next prune; a suppression of the current address and the choices have no period. The member export lists the suppression and the choices (`account.json`), the delivery rows of the account (`activity.json`) and the bounce columns of its invitations; the deletion of an account removes them and the delivery log (cascades). The words of the page, the box, the invitation row, the footer, the policy lines and the sub-processor line are the ones that the owner approved on 2026-11-23.

## Consequences

1. Slice 4c only builds content: a notification mailable extends `NotificationMail` and gets the queue, the limiter, the hold and the retries.
2. Every operator runs one more process. The update notes of `SELF_HOSTING.md` say it, and `app:health` fails when notification mail waits for a worker that does not run.
3. A sign-in link can still wait behind a backlog of `notifications`, because `default` is the last queue of the first worker. This is not new; a queue of its own for level 0 mail is an open point (#1478, risk 1).
4. A crash after the provider accepted a mail and before the job ended can send it twice. The delivery log of slice 4b (#1478) makes it at most once: such a crash leaves the row `sending`, and the mail is not sent again (point 25). The notification stays in the app.
5. Slice 4b depends on the clock of the server (NTP) for the webhook, on an API key with full access for the removal from the list of the provider, and on `APP_KEY` (or `APP_PREVIOUS_KEYS`) for the stop links. Resend disables a webhook that keeps failing, so a wrong secret ends the reports in silence after about a day; nothing checks the time of the last event.
6. The count of this instance does not see the other senders of a shared team. The operator sets `MAIL_MONTHLY_QUOTA` lower than the plan in that case (owner, 2026-11-17).
7. Rules for slice 4c and later. A notification mail goes out only through `queue()`, `later()` or `send()` of the mailer: `sendNow()`, and a Laravel notification that returns the mailable, skip the queue, the hold and the limiter. The refusal for a quota is read only with the mailer `resend`: the `failover` and `roundrobin` mailers throw an exception without the error of the provider, so the hold then comes from the hourly check alone. The `sync` queue driver drops a job that the limiter releases: it is for tests.
8. `MAIL_QUEUE` has two useful values, `mail` and `default`: the worker files read these names. Another name needs a worker that the operator adds.
9. The three values that are numbers (`MAIL_RATE_LIMIT_PER_SECOND`, `MAIL_MONTHLY_QUOTA`, `MAIL_NOTIFICATION_HOLD_PERCENT`) are not cast in `config/mail.php`: an empty value would become 0, which means no limit or a hold at 1%. `App\Support\MailSettings` narrows them and gives the default for an empty or a wrong value.

## Alternatives considered

1. All mail on the queue `mail`: one rule, but a burst of notification mail would make a sign-in link wait behind the limiter, and the existing mails are a handful a day. Design 6.13 point 6 says they are never held.
2. One worker for every queue, with `mail` added to the list: a slow SMTP send (no timeout) would block the live updates. The limiter alone would not be a reason: a release does not block a worker.
3. No hold, only the alert: the quota runs out and the provider refuses the sign-in links too. The owner chose the hold at 95% (decision 6).
4. The hold in the cache, as the design said: a cleared cache would lift the hold, and the check, the workers and the middleware would read two places.
5. The detection of the quota codes in `failed()`: it runs after the job failed and sees only the wrapped message.
