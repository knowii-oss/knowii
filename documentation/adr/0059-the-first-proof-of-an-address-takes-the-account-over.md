# 0059. The first proof of an address takes the account over

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-21
- Pull request: #1707

## Context

An account has an address, and the address is verified or not. An account whose address was never verified belongs to nobody yet: nobody proved that they read its mailbox. The sign-up form makes no account whose address was never verified ([ADR 0065](0065-the-sign-up-makes-the-account-when-the-link-of-its-mail-is-opened.md)).

The decision of the owner of 2026-11-15 ([ADR 0045](0045-openid-connect-sign-in.md), point 14.5) says what happens when a sign-in with a provider proves the address of such an account: the account is taken over. The address is marked as verified, and the password, the second factor, the sessions, the API tokens and the password reset tokens of the account are removed. The person that the provider proved has the account from then on.

A sign-in link ([ADR 0023](0023-sign-in-links.md)) and a password reset prove the same thing as the provider: the person who uses them reads the mailbox of the address. So the three ways get one rule, and one place in the code.

A change of the email address applies only after the new address is confirmed ([ADR 0058](0058-a-change-of-the-email-address-applies-after-its-confirmation.md)): a verified account keeps its verified address until the link of the new address is opened, and the link verifies the new one. So an account whose address is not verified now never had a verified address. No marker of a verification of before is needed: `email_verified_at` tells it.

## Decision

The first proof of an address takes the account over, for every way to prove it. A verified account is never taken over.

1. "Never verified" is `email_verified_at` is null (`User::addressWasNeverVerified()`). A verified account keeps `email_verified_at` through a change of the address (ADR 0058).
2. One action does the takeover: `App\Actions\Auth\TakeOverUnverifiedAccount`, behind the contract `App\Contracts\Auth\TakesOverUnverifiedAccounts`. The sign-in with a provider (`SignInWithOpenIdConnect`), the sign-in link (`MagicLinkController::consume()`) and the password reset (`App\Actions\Fortify\ResetUserPassword`) call it, only for an account whose address was never verified. No other code takes an account over.
3. The caller gives the action the address that was proved: the address of the account when the sign-in link or the reset token was checked, or the address that the provider vouched for. The action reads the row of the account again with `lockForUpdate()` inside its database transaction, and works on that row. The action returns what it did, `App\Enums\KnowiiTakeOverResult`: `TookOver`, `AlreadyVerified`, `OtherAddress` or `Gone`. It changes nothing and returns `OtherAddress` when the row has another address than the proved one, without regard to the case (a correction of the address, [ADR 0058](0058-a-change-of-the-email-address-applies-after-its-confirmation.md) point 5, came in between: the proof is for an address that the account no longer has), `AlreadyVerified` when the row has a verified address (another proof of the same address came first, a moment before), and `Gone` when the row is gone (the account was deleted after the caller read it). The callers decide on the result, never on the model. An account that is gone gets the same answer as another address, the one of a proof that does not work any more (the sign-in link and the reset as in points 6 and 7, the provider with its normal path for an address without an account). Else, in the same transaction:
   1. It marks the address as verified.
   2. It removes the password. A password reset gives the new password, and the account keeps that one only.
   3. It replaces the remember token.
   4. It removes the second factor: the secret, the recovery codes and the date of the confirmation.
   5. It adds 1 to the version of the sessions of the account (point 4), and deletes the rows of the `sessions` table of the account.
   6. It deletes the API tokens and the password reset tokens of the account.
   7. It removes the connections of the account to a provider, with their entitlement keys and the access groups that rules gave because of them (`UnlinksIdentities`, [ADR 0047](0047-entitlement-rules-feed-access-groups.md)).
   8. It clears the proof of a deletion ([ADR 0052](0052-account-deletion-after-a-new-sign-in.md)) and a pending change of the address (ADR 0058).
   9. After the commit, it writes one notice line to the log with the cuid of the account, never the address. The line names the way: `OpenID Connect: took over an account whose address was never verified` (the line of before, for the provider), `Sign-in link: took over …` and `Password reset: took over …`. The lines of the removed connections are also written after the commit.
   10. After the commit, it ends the live connections of the account (point 5).
   11. It returns `TookOver`. After `TookOver`, `AlreadyVerified` and `OtherAddress`, the model of the caller holds the values of the locked row, so that the caller decides on the state that the action saw. After `Gone`, the model of the caller stays as it is.
4. The version of the sessions: a takeover ends every session of the account, whatever the session driver. The column `users.session_version` (an unsigned integer, 0 by default) is the version of the account. The listener `App\Listeners\StoreSessionVersion` writes it into the session at each sign-in: the form with the password, the code of the second factor, a sign-in link, the provider, a new account and the remember cookie (the framework fires `Login` for each of them). The middleware `App\Http\Middleware\EndOutdatedSessions` runs in the `web` group (every page, the routes of Fortify and Jetstream, the authorization of the broadcast channels) and in the `api` group (where only a request of the first-party front-end has a session). It runs before the middleware of the framework that reads the account of the session (it is in the priority list before `AuthenticatesRequests`). A session whose value differs from the version of the account (a session without a value counts as 0) is signed out and invalidated, and the answer is the one of an expired session: a redirect to the sign-in page, 401 for a JSON request. Only the takeover changes the version.
5. The live connections. After the commit, the action calls `App\Contracts\Realtime\EndsLiveConnections` (`App\Support\ReverbLiveConnections`) with the cuid of the account, the identifier of the account for the broadcaster. When the broadcaster of the instance is Reverb, it asks Reverb to end the connections of that user (`POST /apps/{app}/users/{user}/terminate_connections` of the Pusher HTTP API). Reverb knows the user of a connection from the moment that the connection joins a presence channel: a member who shows the online status joins one on each page of a community ([ADR 0027](0027-presence-channels.md)). A connection that joined no presence channel (a member who hides the online status, or a tab that is on no page of a community) is not ended by this call: it keeps the private channels that it joined before the takeover until the tab closes or loses the connection. The next authorization of a channel by that tab is refused (point 4). Another broadcaster gets no call. The call waits 2 seconds at most, to connect and to get the answer (`ReverbLiveConnections::TIMEOUT_SECONDS`): it runs in the request of the person who proved the address. A broadcaster that does not answer in time, or answers with an error, is one warning line in the log, never an error of the request, and the takeover stays.
6. The sign-in link. For an account whose address was never verified, `consume()` calls the action. The second factor of before is gone then, so the person is signed in without a challenge. The session of the person who uses the link stays: it becomes a session of the account after the takeover, with the new version. Every other account is handled as before: the link replaces the password, never the second factor ([ADR 0023](0023-sign-in-links.md), point 4), and the landing of [ADR 0057](0057-the-landing-after-the-address-check-travels-in-the-link.md) does not change. When the action returns `AlreadyVerified`, the link goes on in the same way. When it returns `OtherAddress` or `Gone`, the link signs nobody in, and the answer is the one of a link that expired or was used. Before it signs in, the sign-in link reads the account again; an account that is gone signs nobody in.
7. The password reset. The broker of the framework checks the token of the reset mail first. For an account whose address was never verified, `ResetUserPassword` calls the action with the new password: the account keeps the new password only, the address is verified, and the rest of point 3 applies. The person then signs in with the new password, as after every reset. Every other account gets the new password, and nothing else changes; so does an account for which the action returns `AlreadyVerified`. When it returns `OtherAddress` or `Gone`, the reset changes nothing, and the answer is the one of a token that does not work ("This password reset token is invalid.").
8. The sign-in with a provider calls the same action for an account whose address was never verified ([ADR 0045](0045-openid-connect-sign-in.md), point 14.5). The takeover comes first, then the connection to the account of the provider: the connection of the person who signs in is the only one that the account has after it. A verified account is connected as before (case 3 of `SignInWithOpenIdConnect`): the password, the second factor, the tokens and the other connections stay, and the challenge of the second factor comes. So is an account for which the action returns `AlreadyVerified`. When it returns `OtherAddress` or `Gone` (the rows are locked by their address in the transaction of the sign-in, so this does not happen), the address of the provider takes its normal path again, once. A pending address of an account (ADR 0058) is not its address: the provider finds no account for it.
9. The link of the address check does not run the action. The person who opens it is signed in as the account already: after the normal sign-up, that is the person who made the account.
10. An account whose address was never verified cannot change its password and cannot turn the second factor on. The change of the password (`App\Actions\Fortify\UpdateUserPassword`) gets the error of a wrong current password. "Enable" of the second factor (`App\Actions\Fortify\EnableTwoFactorAuthentication`) and new recovery codes (`App\Actions\Fortify\GenerateNewRecoveryCodes`) get the refusal of the framework (403), and store nothing. The confirmation of the second factor (`App\Actions\Fortify\ConfirmTwoFactorAuthentication`; the confirmation is the step that turns it on) gets the error of a wrong code. The four replace the actions of Fortify, and read the model of the request: a request that passed the session check before a takeover stores nothing after it. The address check, a sign-in link or a password reset comes first.
11. A challenge of the second factor whose account has no second factor any more (it was turned off, or a takeover removed it) ends: `App\Http\Requests\TwoFactorLoginRequest` (which replaces the request of Fortify) forgets `login.id`, `login.remember` and the landing that a sign-in link held for it, and the page and the answer of the challenge redirect to the sign-in page.
12. A sign-in link works for the address that it was sent to ([ADR 0058](0058-a-change-of-the-email-address-applies-after-its-confirmation.md), point 19): `MagicLinks` gives no account for a link when the account has another address now. So a sign-in link always shows that the person reads the mailbox of the address that the account has now, and the address that the link gives the action (point 3) is that address.
13. No screen changes, and no word of a screen changes.
14. A request that read the account before a takeover writes nothing after it. Every update of a `users` row by a model also needs the version of the sessions that the model read (`User::setKeysForSaveQuery()` adds `where session_version = <the original value of the model>`; a model that does not hold the column, from a query that selected some columns, is not checked). A save that matches no row writes nothing, without an error. The deletion of the account with a password (`App\Actions\DeleteUser::delete()`) gets the model of the request from the web and API controllers, reads the row again with `lockForUpdate()` in its transaction, and deletes nothing when that row has another version of the sessions than the model: the answer is the one of a wrong password ("The password is incorrect."). The takeover writes the new version on the locked row, so its own write matches. A new account holds the version 0 from its first write. The writers checked: the actions of the application (the profile form, the correction and the change of the address, the password change and the reset, the second factor, the time zone, the online status, the pop-ups, the avatar, the proof of a deletion, the sign-up), the remember token of the framework (at a sign-in with "remember me", at a sign-out, after a reset), the password that the framework hashes again at a sign-in and in "Log out other browser sessions" (`logoutOtherDevices`), the recovery code that a challenge uses up, and the writes of Fortify and Jetstream for the second factor. None of them writes with a model of another version on purpose: the callers of the takeover hold the values of the locked row after it. A request of "Log out other browser sessions" that was under way during a takeover can still end the sessions of the new owner (the rows of the `sessions` table of the account): they sign in again, and the request gives no access to the account.
15. The correction of an address that was never verified (ADR 0058, point 5) and the link of the address check (ADR 0058, point 18) work on the row too: the correction locks it and waits as a pending address only while it has no verified address, and the address check writes only while the row has the address of the link and no verified address. The correction is a pending address now: the account keeps its address until the link of the new address is opened, and a takeover clears the pending address (point 3, step 8). With point 3, a proof of the address and a correction end in one of two states, whatever their order: the proof is for the address of the row and takes the account over (the pending address goes), or the link of the correction came first, the row has the new address, verified, and the proof of the address of before changes nothing.

## Consequences

1. After a takeover by a sign-in link, the account has no password, as after a takeover by a provider ([ADR 0045](0045-openid-connect-sign-in.md), point 18). The person signs in with a link, or sets a password with "Forgot your password?".
2. After a password reset of an account whose address was never verified, the address is verified: the reset mail proved the mailbox, as the mail of the address check does.
3. The name, the username, the profile and the personal community of the account stay, as with a provider. The person changes them in the profile settings.
4. The link of the address check is not concerned (point 9).
5. A verified account is never taken over. A change of its address waits for its confirmation and leaves `email_verified_at` as it is (ADR 0058).
6. A takeover ends every session of the account, whatever the session driver: each one is signed out at its next request. The sessions that exist when this change is deployed hold no value: they count as 0, the version of every account, and go on.
7. A takeover ends the live connections of the account that Reverb knows. A connection that joined no presence channel stays until its tab closes (point 5).
8. Two proofs of the same address at the same time: the first one takes the account over, and the second one goes on as for a verified account (points 6 to 8). No error page. A proof of an address that a correction replaced a moment before takes nothing over, and answers as for a link that does not work.
9. A person who signed up and did not verify the address yet sees the error of a wrong current password when they change the password, a refusal when they turn the second factor on or ask for new recovery codes, and the error of a wrong code when they confirm the second factor. They verify the address first.
10. A new way to prove an address calls the same action. A new thing that signs in or acts for an account (a passkey, a device) is added to point 3 and to the action in the same change.
11. Tests: `tests/Feature/FirstProofOfAddressTest.php` covers the sign-in link, the password reset, the provider, the link of the address check, a member whose change of the address waits, the refusals of point 10, the challenge of point 11, the action, two proofs at the same time, a proof and a correction of the address in both orders, a proof for an account that is gone, the writes of a model read before the takeover (point 14) and of a model read after it, the deletion of the account by a request that read it before the takeover, the live connections with the timeout and the migration. `tests/Feature/SessionVersionTest.php` covers the sessions with the `database` and the `array` drivers, with the cookie of the session and with the remember cookie, on a page, on a route of Fortify, on the API of the session and on the authorization of a channel. `tests/Feature/OpenIdConnectTest.php` (case 4) covers the provider.

## Alternatives considered

1. The takeover in each of the three places: three copies of a list that must stay the same.
2. A sign-in link or a password reset that is refused until the address is verified: the person who owns the address could not get into the account, because the link of the address check needs a sign-in first.
3. A new, empty account for the person who proves the address: the address is unique, so the account of before would need another address or a deletion, with its content. The decision of 2026-11-15 chose the takeover.
4. The takeover also resets the name, the username and the profile: a later question. The decision of 2026-11-15 keeps them.
5. A marker "verified once" on the account, beside `email_verified_at`: it was needed while a change of the address cleared `email_verified_at` at once. With ADR 0058 the change waits for its confirmation, and the marker would say nothing more than `email_verified_at`.
6. An error when two proofs come at the same time: the second person proved the same mailbox, and gets the answer of a verified account instead.
